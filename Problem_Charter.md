## Strategic problem

talabat is a publicly listed (Dubai Financial Market, IPO priced December 2024), Group-wide on-demand
delivery platform operating across eight markets, with FY2025 GMV of USD 9.5 billion (+28% cFX),
revenue of USD 3.9 billion, Adjusted EBITDA of USD 615 million (6.5% margin), and 7.5 million active
customers. For 2026, talabat's Board approved a USD 175 million investment programme, split into two
components the company itself discloses: approximately USD 120 million for "Everyday App" initiatives
(talabat pro, AI personalisation, multi-vertical cross-sell, grocery/retail expansion, new ventures)
and approximately USD 55 million for Food-leadership initiatives (restaurant-partner growth, food
customer value proposition, delivery reliability). talabat has not disclosed, and this project cannot
observe, how that programme should be allocated *within* each component, across markets that differ
sharply in maturity, profitability, and competitive intensity — Egypt, for instance, only became a
standalone profitable segment in FY2025, while Q1 2026 Group-wide Adjusted EBITDA margin compressed
to 4.8% from 6.3% a year earlier, evidence that not every dollar of this programme is earning the
same return.

**The decision this plan answers:** how should talabat allocate its 2026 USD 175 million investment
programme across Everyday App and Food-leadership initiatives — and within them, across markets and
initiative categories — to maximise profitable growth, customer lifetime value, customer retention,
and long-term platform economics, given the evidence actually available and without pretending to
possess internal customer-level data this project does not have?

## Why this problem matters, and why now

talabat's own results show real dispersion of return, not a uniform growth story: some markets and
mechanisms are already proven (talabat pro's Group/GCC order-frequency and retention uplift; the
adtech-personalisation layer's EBITDA contribution rising from over USD 14 million in FY2024 to over
USD 30 million in FY2025), while others carry acknowledged pressure (Group-wide margin compression;
currency and inflation exposure in specific markets; well-funded local competitors in individual
markets, Egypt among them). A USD 175 million programme allocated without an explicit, evidence-ranked
framework risks funding what is already familiar rather than what the disclosed evidence actually
supports — the exact failure mode a capital-allocation discipline exists to prevent. This matters
now specifically because 2026 is the programme's live budget year: the allocation decision is not
hypothetical, it is the one talabat's own management is making this year, with a mid-year checkpoint
already visible in the Q1 2026 results this project's corpus includes.

## Operating-market scope

**Primary scope: talabat Group across its eight operating markets.** Evidence is used at the level it
is actually reported — Group, GCC, non-GCC, or country-specific — and never silently promoted or
demoted between levels (see the Geographic Evidence Rules this charter's OS enforces). "MENA" is not
used as a stand-in for talabat's own market set. Egypt remains a genuine, evidenced worked example
throughout — it is the corpus's richest single-country data point (a full standalone segment P&L as
of FY2025) — but it is one input to a Group-wide allocation decision, not the decision's boundary.

## Role of AI in this plan

The Agentic OS synthesizes talabat's disclosed evidence into a structured knowledge base; ranks
candidate investment initiatives against explicit, documented criteria; builds scenario-based
financial forecasts (base/upside/downside, not single-point estimates); monitors the KPI and stage-gate
framework that would tell management whether an initiative is working; and recommends how capital
might be reallocated as new evidence arrives. **The AI does not claim to possess talabat's internal,
customer-level data, and its output is not a substitute for management decision-making** — every
allocation recommendation in the resulting plan is a range or a staged-funding proposal grounded in
disclosed evidence and explicitly labeled assumptions, gated for human approval before any capital
actually moves, never a false-precision single number presented as if it were internally verified.

## Available evidence

talabat's own disclosures: the FY2024 and FY2025 annual reports, eight quarterly financial-statement
sets, quarterly earnings/investor presentations, the Capital Markets Day materials, the IPO Offering
Memorandum and related regulatory filings, and sell-side consensus estimates — a 29-document primary
corpus, already ingested and cited to page level in this OS's knowledge base. Secondary evidence
(market-research overviews, competitor landscape summaries, official newsroom items) is used only
where explicitly labeled as such and ranked below the primary corpus.

## Evidence limitations

talabat does not disclose customer-level data, initiative-by-initiative cost or return figures, or a
country-by-country breakdown of the USD 175 million programme. **Exact optimal allocations cannot be
proven from public data alone**, and this plan does not claim otherwise: recommendations are expressed
as ranges and staged-funding proposals, financial projections are scenario-based with explicit
confidence labels, and every planning input not directly disclosed is logged as a registered
assumption rather than presented as fact. Where a KPI has no disclosed baseline, the plan says so
explicitly rather than inventing a target.

## Expected Business Plan output

The graded 14-section GSB Template v2.0 (McKinsey Edition) structure is unchanged and immutable (see
`AI_Business_Plan_Template.md` and the Template Compliance Gate) — this pivot changes the business
question the fourteen sections answer, not the template itself. The resulting plan will name and rank
candidate investment initiatives, recommend allocation ranges (not falsely precise point figures),
specify pilot sequencing and stage gates, connect each recommendation to the value drivers it is
expected to move (order frequency, multi-vertical engagement, GMV, revenue, gross profit, EBITDA, cash
flow), name the risks and evidence gaps honestly, and specify how management would measure success and
reallocate capital as real performance data arrives.
