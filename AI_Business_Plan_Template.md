This template defines the structure of the AI Business Plan for the Agentic OS Capstone Project. Version 2.0 keeps the fourteen sections of the original template and embeds McKinsey problem-solving and communication concepts into each one, so the plan you produce is not a description of an idea but a structured argument for a decision.

## The five McKinsey concepts this template is built on

- **Pyramid Principle** — every section leads with its answer, then supports it with grouped arguments, then evidence. The reader should be able to stop after the first paragraph of any section and still know your conclusion.
- **SCQA** — the Executive Summary follows Situation, Complication, Question, Answer. The Answer is your recommendation, and it appears on page one.
- **MECE** — every breakdown (segments, options, risks, cost drivers) must be mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive: no overlaps, no gaps.
- **Hypothesis-driven analysis** — state what you believe early, then use the evidence in your Agentic OS vault to prove or kill it. Analysis without a hypothesis is a data tour.
- **Value driver logic** — financials and KPIs cascade from a driver tree: how, mechanically, does the AI solution create value? Every number in Section 9 and every KPI in Section 13 should sit somewhere on that tree.

> **McKINSEY LENS · Writing discipline throughout:** one message per exhibit; action titles on every chart ('Churn is concentrated in prepaid youth segments', not 'Churn analysis'); the 80/20 rule on depth — go deep only where the decision turns.

## 1. Executive Summary

> **McKINSEY LENS · Write** this section last, place it first, and structure it as SCQA. The Answer — your recommendation and its expected impact — must appear within the first half page. If leadership reads nothing else, this section must be sufficient to decide.

- Situation — the business, its market position, and the strategic context in one short paragraph
- Complication — the specific problem or opportunity that makes action necessary now
- Question — the decision this plan answers
- Answer — the recommended AI solution, the expected impact (quantified), and the investment required
- Business name and overview; mission and vision statements
- Description of the AI product/service, target market, and value proposition
- Key objectives, growth goals, and summary of financial projections and funding needs

## 2. Business Description

> **McKINSEY LENS · Define** the problem before the solution, and state your governing hypothesis explicitly: 'We believe [AI intervention] will [impact] because [logic].' The rest of the plan exists to prove or refine that sentence. Decompose the problem MECE so the reader sees you have covered the whole space, not cherry-picked.

- Industry overview and opportunities for AI adoption
- Problem statement — the specific problem(s) solved by the AI solution, decomposed MECE into its root drivers
- Governing hypothesis — the one-sentence claim this plan will prove
- Core features and benefits of the AI product/service
- Business model (e.g., SaaS, licensing, consulting, internal capability)
- Current business stage (startup, scale-up, established)

## 3. Market Analysis

> **McKINSEY LENS · Size** the market top-down AND bottom-up and reconcile the two — a single unsourced figure is not sizing. Segment MECE. Apply 80/20: identify the minority of segments that carry the majority of the value, and say so. All figures must reflect the Egyptian/MENA context of your chosen company and cite the vault note they came from.

**Industry Trends**
- AI market size and growth projections — top-down (analyst reports) triangulated with bottom-up (customers × adoption × price)
- Trends in AI adoption by sector (e.g., fintech, healthcare, logistics, telecom) in the MENA region

**Target Market**
- MECE segmentation with customer profiles and segment sizing
- Early adopters and primary users; the 20% of segments driving 80% of value

**Competitive Analysis**
- SWOT analysis with a 'so what' conclusion per quadrant — no orphan observations
- Competitor benchmarking (capabilities, pricing, customer base)

**Opportunities**
- Untapped markets or underserved industries
- Technological advancements that enable differentiation

## 4. Value Proposition

> **McKINSEY LENS · Every** claim here must survive the 'so what?' test twice: so what for the customer, so what for the P&L. Quantify or delete. This section is the top of your value driver tree — the mechanisms named here must reappear as line items in Section 9 and KPIs in Section 13.

- Unique Selling Proposition (USP) of the AI solution
- Value creation mechanisms — efficiency, cost savings, revenue lift, risk reduction, insight — each quantified
- Impact on customer outcomes and return on investment (ROI), with the calculation logic shown

## 5. AI Technology and Development

> **McKINSEY LENS · Technology** choices are business decisions: present build vs. buy vs. partner as a structured options analysis with explicit criteria (cost, speed, capability, data confidentiality), not a tools list. Depth here follows 80/20 — detail only the components on which the value case depends.

- Description of AI technology (e.g., machine learning, LLMs, natural language processing, computer vision)
- Build / buy / partner decision with criteria and rationale
- Proprietary algorithms, data models, or patents
- Infrastructure and tools (cloud platforms, AI frameworks, agentic tooling / MCP where relevant)
- Scalability and adaptability of the AI solution
- Research and development plans (future features, updates)

## 6. Business Model and Revenue Streams

> **McKINSEY LENS · Revenue** streams must be MECE — no double counting between streams — and each stream must trace to a branch of the value driver tree. State the unit economics: what one customer, transaction, or deployment earns and costs.

- Revenue generation methods: subscription fees, licensing, service contracts, custom AI solutions, internal cost savings
- Pricing strategy (tiered plans, enterprise pricing, value-based pricing)
- Recurring vs. one-time revenue streams; unit economics per customer/deployment

## 7. Marketing and Sales Strategy

> **McKINSEY LENS · Structure** acquisition as funnel economics — awareness to conversion to retention, with a number at each stage — and concentrate spend where Section 3's 80/20 analysis says the value is. Positioning is a choice; state what you are NOT targeting and why.

**Positioning**
- How the AI product stands out — and the segments deliberately not pursued

**Customer Acquisition**
- Channels (digital marketing, partnerships, events) with expected funnel conversion at each stage
- Sales funnel and customer journey mapping

**Partnerships**
- Collaborations with tech companies, data providers, or resellers

**Retention**
- Customer support, training, and community-building
- Regular updates and enhancements to the AI solution

## 8. Operations Plan

> **McKINSEY LENS · Test** the operating model against the 7S framework — Strategy, Structure, Systems, Shared values, Skills, Style, Staff — and name the misalignments honestly. An AI plan that ignores the organization that must run it is the most common failure pattern in the case literature.

- Infrastructure needs (data storage, cloud computing, hardware)
- Development and maintenance workflow
- Key team roles (data scientists, developers, product managers) and the skills gap vs. today
- 7S alignment check — where the organization is and is not ready, and what closes the gap
- Security and compliance measures (data protection law, privacy regulations)

## 9. Financial Plan

> **McKINSEY LENS · Build** the numbers as a value driver tree, not a spreadsheet of hopeful rows: recommendation → value drivers → operational metrics → financial line items. Present three scenarios (base / upside / downside) and list the assumptions each one turns on. An estimated number with visible logic beats a precise number with none.

- Value driver tree linking the AI solution to financial outcomes
- Initial investment and operational costs
- Revenue forecasts (monthly/annual) in base, upside, and downside scenarios
- Break-even analysis; profit and loss projections
- Key assumptions register — the five to ten numbers the case depends on
- Funding requirements and allocation; monetization strategy and scaling costs

## 10. Risk Analysis

> **McKINSEY LENS · Structure** risks MECE (technical / market / financial / organizational / regulatory), place them on a probability-impact matrix, and run a pre-mortem: 'It is two years from now and this initiative failed — why?' The pre-mortem findings become your mitigation priorities.

- Technical risks (algorithm bias, scalability, model drift)
- Market risks (competition, adoption barriers)
- Financial risks (cost overruns, revenue shortfalls)
- Organizational and regulatory risks
- Probability-impact matrix and pre-mortem findings
- Mitigation strategy per material risk, with an owner

## 11. Corporate Social Responsibility & Responsible AI

> **McKINSEY LENS · Treat** responsible AI as risk management and license-to-operate, not decoration: connect each commitment to a specific risk in Section 10 or a stakeholder expectation. Vague virtue costs credibility; specific governance earns it.

- Ethical considerations in AI development (bias, transparency, fairness) tied to concrete controls
- Sustainability initiatives
- Commitment to responsible AI practices and governance structures

## 12. Implementation Plan

> **McKINSEY LENS · Structure** the roadmap on the Three Horizons: H1 — quick wins that fund credibility (0–6 months), H2 — the core build (6–18 months), H3 — scaling and new options (18+ months). Sequence by dependency and by proof: each horizon should generate the evidence that justifies the next.

- Three Horizons roadmap with milestones (pilot, launch, scaling phases)
- Development timelines and dependencies
- Marketing rollout plan
- Team expansion and recruitment goals

## 13. Monitoring and Evaluation

> **McKINSEY LENS · KPIs** are the leaves of the value driver tree — if a metric does not connect to a driver in Section 9, it is vanity. Distinguish leading indicators (adoption, usage) from lagging ones (revenue, churn), and define the review cadence and the kill criteria: what evidence would make you stop.

- KPIs mapped to the value driver tree: customer acquisition cost (CAC), monthly recurring revenue (MRR), churn rate, product usage metrics
- Leading vs. lagging indicator split
- Tools and methods for performance tracking
- Feedback loops for continuous improvement; kill / pivot criteria

## 14. Appendices

> **McKINSEY LENS · The** appendix is the ghost deck: the backup evidence behind every headline claim. One exhibit, one message, one action title. Include the traceability note — how each major claim maps to a vault note and its source document in your Agentic OS.

- Supporting research and data; traceability notes (claim → vault note → source)
- Resumes of key team members
- Technical specifications of the AI product
- Case studies or pilot program results
- Legal and compliance documents
