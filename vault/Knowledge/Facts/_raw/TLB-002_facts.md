### GMV

- GMV by segment (FY2025 pro forma): GCC USD 6,332mn (+20% y/y, 82% of GMV vs. 85% in 2024);
  non-GCC USD 1,096mn (+42% y/y). (TLB-002, page 18)
- GMV by vertical (FY2025 pro forma): Food USD 6,652mn (+20% y/y); Groceries & Retail (G&R)
  USD 2,768mn (+47% y/y). (TLB-002, page 18)
- Narrative (unreconciled with table above): "core GCC markets... growing GMV by 22% year-on-year to
  USD 7.7 billion, while the non-GCC segment... 57% expansion to USD 1.7 billion." (TLB-002, page 18)
- FY2024 comparative GMV: USD 7,428mn. (TLB-002, page 18)
- Food GMV reached USD 6.7bn (+20% y/y); Restaurant Active Partners ~71,000 (+21% y/y). (TLB-002, page 14)
- G&R GMV reached USD 2.8bn (+47% y/y), of which talabat mart GMV reached USD 1.3bn (+45% y/y). (TLB-002, page 14)
- talabat mart GMV (at-a-glance figure): USD 1.25bn, fulfilled by ~160 dark stores. (TLB-002, page 12)
- 2025 GMV per country map: talabat processes USD 9.4bn in GMV via 7.7mn customers placing 585mn
  orders with 84k partners. (TLB-002, page 12)

### Orders
- 585 million orders placed on the platform in 2025. (TLB-002, page 12)
- Average orders per active user per month: 6.7, a 5% increase in frequency y/y. (TLB-002, page 9)
- 90 meal occasions per month referenced as the addressable demand pool talabat targets a larger
  share of. (TLB-002, page 14)

### Monthly Active Customers
- Monthly Active Users (MAU): ~7.7 million for December 2025, +18% y/y. (TLB-002, pages 5, 9)
- Multi-vertical customers exceed one-third of the user base, driving over 70% of total GMV. (TLB-002, page 14)

### Talabat Pro
- talabat pro subscriber adoption more than doubled in 2025 (3.2x user growth); subscribers now
  account for nearly 50% of talabat's total GMV. (TLB-002, pages 4, 10, 13)
- Expanded to all eight operating markets in 2025, including strategic launches in Egypt (February
  2025) and Iraq (September 2025). (TLB-002, page 14)
- Benefits: free delivery, booster discounts on Food best-sellers, discounts on hundreds of key
  value items within talabat mart, on-time guarantee, priority customer support, dine-out discounts,
  partner services (streaming, ride-hailing). (TLB-002, page 13)
- 2026 focus: strengthening talabat pro "as a multi-vertical engagement engine," with incremental
  investment in programmed benefits, free delivery co-funding, campaign offers, and partner
  services. (TLB-002, page 14)
- Subscription revenue (at-a-glance): USD 74mn (2025). (TLB-002, page 11)
- Subscription fees & Other Income combined line (pro forma FY2025): USD 1,397mn (+47% y/y). (TLB-002, page 20)
- Audited subscription fees (since-inception period): USD 83,790,496. (TLB-002, page 105)

### Advertising
- Consolidated AdTech (advertising and listing fees) revenue: USD 323mn (+32% y/y), 3.4%–3.5% of
  GMV. (TLB-002, pages 11, 14, 19)
- In the UAE, advertising technology margins have already reached 7% of GMV for talabat mart. (TLB-002, page 10)
- AdTech infrastructure allows Partners to bid for premium in-app visibility; FMCG manufacturers can
  execute retail media campaigns within the grocery vertical. (TLB-002, page 12)

### Q-Commerce
- talabat mart (proprietary dark stores) GMV: USD 1.3bn (+45% y/y), or USD 1.25bn per at-a-glance
  figure; ~160 stores across all 8 markets. (TLB-002, pages 12, 14)
- talabat mart revenue take rate: GMV flows to revenue at an effective ~95% take rate; talabat mart
  was 31% of total revenue in 2025 vs. 28% in 2024. (TLB-002, page 20)
- talabat mart stores use data-led procurement and optimised layouts, processing orders in minutes. (TLB-002, page 12)
- 2026 investment priorities for talabat mart: improving affordability, increasing store density,
  expanding supply chain infrastructure. (TLB-002, page 10)

### Customer Growth
- MAU grew from prior year to ~7.7mn, +18% y/y. (TLB-002, page 9)
- Active Partners grew to ~84,000, +22% y/y (of which ~71,000 restaurant partners, +21% y/y). (TLB-002, pages 5, 10, 14)
- Active riders grew to ~157,000, +27% y/y. (TLB-002, pages 5, 10, 14)
- Customer Acquisition Costs (CAC, internal classification, part of CARC): USD 67mn customer
  marketing costs in 2025 (2024: USD 44mn per FY24 Financial Review comparative table, page 21). (TLB-002, page 21)

### Retention
- "We have expanded our ecosystem, focusing on high-value customer retention through our talabat pro
  subscription programme and multi-vertical offerings." (TLB-002, page 5)
- "Deploy targeted capital to defend core markets and retain high and medium value customers against
  partial or complete churn to competition" — explicit 2026 strategic objective. (TLB-002, page 14)
- Multi-vertical users "show significantly higher order frequency and retention." (TLB-002, pages 7–8, 12)
- "It takes approximately six orders for our machine learning models to develop personalised
  recommendations... This enhanced personalisation helps customers quickly find what they need,
  benefit from relevant deals, and ultimately drives higher retention and order frequency." (TLB-002, page 15)
- Customer Acquisition and Retention Costs (CARC), internal metric: USD 155mn / 1.6% of GMV in 2025
  (2024: USD 103mn / 1.4% of GMV) — combines talabat-funded vouchering (USD 89mn) and customer
  marketing costs (USD 67mn). (TLB-002, page 21)
- Goodwill impairment cohort models "analyse the past order behaviour of (local) end customers... key
  inputs of the cohort models include the customer retention/reorder rate, customer activity rate,
  average order size, and commission rates." (TLB-002, page 100)
- Following a 5-day administrative closure in Qatar in September 2025, "restoring order volumes and
  demonstrating the strength of our operational capabilities, the stickiness of our platform and the
  loyalty of our customers." (TLB-002, page 9)

### Subscription

- Embedded FinTech: digital wallets for rapid refunds/corporate allowances; talabat Postpaid for

### Logistics
- Third-party logistics (3PL) model; advanced dispatch engine using real-time geolocational data;
  predictive analytics for merchant preparation times; dynamic route optimisation for order
  stacking. (TLB-002, page 12)
- 157k rider fleet paid USD 1.5bn in 2025 delivery costs. (TLB-002, page 11)
- Rider staffing algorithm (licensed from Delivery Hero) translates demand forecasts into optimised
  shift schedules. (TLB-002, page 16)
- Rider Safety Score: 94% (December 2025), reflecting average compliance with speed, harsh riding,
  and phone-use guidelines. (TLB-002, page 15)
- EV fleet: UAE 240 electric vehicles (Jan 2026); Egypt 200 electric vehicles (end 2025, deployed
  in Cairo, talabat's inaugural Egypt EV programme, scaling planned for 2026); electric delivery
  pilots also active in Jordan and Oman. (TLB-002, pages 42–44)
- Delivery vehicles account for approximately 43% of talabat's emissions; total emissions
  875,157 tCO2e in 2025 (Scope 1: 8,177; Scope 2: 8,847; Scope 3: 858,132 tCO2e). (TLB-002, pages 41–42)
- Riders earned over USD 21.5 million in tips in 2025, keeping 100% of tips. (TLB-002, pages 33, 35)

### AI
- "By leveraging artificial intelligence to enhance vendor onboarding and customer personalisation,
  we are driving operational efficiency and improving the user experience." (TLB-002, page 7)
- Machine learning curates personalised customer recommendations; ~6 orders needed for models to
  personalise; recommendation system extended from cuisines to items (e.g., "Meal for One"). (TLB-002, page 15)
- Personalisation algorithm estimated to contribute USD 30mn+ incremental EBITDA p.a. (2025 estimate). (TLB-002, page 15)
- AI used for internal operations automation (invoice matching, sales lead qualification),
  customer-facing item tagging, and preparation-time algorithms optimising delivery times. (TLB-002, page 16)
- Predictive dispatch algorithms and dynamic route optimisation minimise delivery times and maximise
  courier efficiency. (TLB-002, page 8)
- 2025 experiment: expanded targeted deals to optimise Partner advertising spend, "leading to
  savings while keeping the acquisitions constant." (TLB-002, page 15)
- Access to Delivery Hero's global tech-stack and back-end solutions cited as a key innovation
  advantage; talabat processes over 235 terabytes of data daily. (TLB-002, pages 7, 16)
- 2026: AI to continue driving personalisation and smart targeting for consumers and vendors,
  "particularly within our rapidly growing adtech business." (TLB-002, page 10)

### Competition
- "food delivery market share is more than one to more than ten times larger than our closest peer"
  across the 8 markets (Redseer Consulting data). (TLB-002, page 8)
- Country-level relative food-service category share vs. nearest competitor: Jordan 4x+, Egypt
  10x+, Iraq (unlabelled multiplier shown alongside), Oman 2x+, Qatar 10x+, Bahrain 1x+, UAE 8x+,
  Kuwait 4x+/7x+ (map figures). (TLB-002, page 5)
- talabat's market penetration: roughly 20–25% for foodservice and 2–3% for groceries — implying
  large structural headroom. (TLB-002, page 8)
- Competitive risks cited: traditional offline restaurants/shops building delivery capability,
  technology giants and well-funded entrants, "SuperApps," and quick commerce providers; ongoing
  market consolidation creating larger, better-resourced competitors. (TLB-002, page 25)
- Antitrust/competition-law investigations ongoing in Kuwait and the UAE as of report date. (TLB-002, page 26)
- Qatar 5-day administrative closure in September 2025 due to regulatory action; talabat "executed
  swiftly, restoring order volumes." (TLB-002, page 9)
- Heightened competition across several key markets in 2025 cited as a headwind alongside some
  consumer-spending moderation and a shifting regulatory backdrop (higher GCC corporate tax,
  sector-specific platform regulation). (TLB-002, page 9)
