## Executive Summary
This is talabat's FY2025 Integrated Annual Report, the most current and authoritative document in
the corpus, covering strategic report, governance report, and audited consolidated financial
statements. talabat operates as the leading on-demand delivery platform in the MENA region across
eight countries — UAE, Kuwait, Egypt, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, Jordan, and Iraq — serving ~7.7 million
monthly active customers via ~84,000 active Partners and ~157,000 active riders. FY2025 GMV reached
USD 9.421bn (+27% y/y reported, +28% constant currency), with management revenue of USD 3.876bn
(+31%), Adjusted EBITDA of USD 615mn (6.5% margin), and net income of USD 464mn (4.9% margin). The
company completed a CEO transition in November 2025 (Toon Gyssels succeeding Tomaso Rodriguez) and
raised its total 2025 dividend to USD 421mn (90% payout). Strategically, 2025 centered on
strengthening the core food delivery Consumer Value Proposition, scaling the Groceries & Retail
vertical (talabat mart), and "turbo-charging customer loyalty" via the talabat pro subscription
programme, which more than doubled its adoption rate and expanded to all eight markets, including a
notable February 2025 launch in Egypt. Critically, the audited financial statements' segment note
(Note 31) discloses that Egypt independently met the quantitative thresholds for a reportable
IFRS 8 segment in 2025 and is now broken out separately from the "non-GCC" bucket (which retains
only Jordan and Iraq) — giving this document unusually granular, audited Egypt-specific financial
disclosure not typically available in talabat's reporting.

## Key Operational Metrics
- Monthly Active Customers (MAU): ~7.7 million (December 2025), +18% y/y (TLB-002, pages 5, 9)
- Orders: 585 million orders placed in 2025 (TLB-002, page 12)
- Order frequency: average 6.7 orders per month per active user, +5% y/y (TLB-002, page 9)
- Active Partners: ~84,000 (+22% y/y); of which ~71,000 restaurant Active Partners (+21% y/y) (TLB-002, pages 5, 10, 14)
- Active riders: ~157,000 (+27% y/y) (TLB-002, pages 5, 10, 14)
- talabat mart: ~160 dark stores across all 8 markets; talabat mart GMV USD 1.3bn (+45% y/y) / USD 1.25bn per at-a-glance figure (TLB-002, pages 12, 14)
- Multi-vertical customers: more than one-third of active customers order across both food and
  grocery; these customers account for over 70% of total GMV (TLB-002, page 10)
- Partner-funded savings: USD 588mn (6.2% of GMV) (2024: ~USD 450mn / 6.1% of GMV) (TLB-002, page 14)
- AdTech (advertising and listing) revenue: USD 323mn (3.4%–3.5% of GMV) (TLB-002, pages 11, 14, 19)
- Subscription revenue: USD 74mn (2025, at-a-glance figure) (TLB-002, page 11)
- Employees: 7,821 total (talabat-only, excluding instashop) as of 31 Dec 2025, across 90+ nationalities;
  436 tech (product/design/engineering/data) FTEs; 157k rider fleet paid USD 1.5bn in 2025 delivery costs (TLB-002, pages 5, 11, 16)
- Data processed: 235 terabytes daily (TLB-002, page 15)
- Rider Safety Score: 94% (December 2025) (TLB-002, page 15)
- talabat pro: subscriber base grew 3.2x in 2025; subscribers now account for ~50% of GMV (TLB-002, pages 4, 13)
- Personalisation algorithm: estimated incremental EBITDA contribution of USD 30mn+ p.a. (2025 estimate) (TLB-002, page 15)

## Retention-Relevant Information
- talabat pro is explicitly positioned as the group's central retention/loyalty lever: "the talabat
  pro subscription programme acts as a multi-vertical engagement engine... These benefits drive a
  material uplift in order frequency." Benefits include free delivery, booster discounts on Food
  best-sellers, discounts on hundreds of key value items within talabat mart, on-time guarantee,
  priority customer support, dine-out discounts, and partner services (streaming, ride-hailing) (TLB-002, page 13)
- talabat pro adoption more than doubled in 2025 (3.2x user growth); subscribers now ~50% of GMV;
  expanded to all 8 markets including Egypt (Feb 2025 launch) and Iraq (Sept 2025 launch) (TLB-002, pages 4, 10, 13–14, 19)
- "Deploy targeted capital to defend core markets and retain high and medium value customers against
  partial or complete churn to competition" is listed as an explicit 2026 strategic objective under
  the "Bolstering Food vertical's competitive positioning" pillar (TLB-002, page 14)
- Multi-vertical engagement drives retention: "the one-third of our users engaging across multiple
  verticals show significantly higher order frequency and retention" (TLB-002, page 8); "Customers
  engaging across both food and grocery demonstrate significantly higher order frequency and
  retention, deepening platform reliance and amplifying lifetime value" (TLB-002, page 12)
- AI/personalisation for retention: "It takes approximately six orders for our machine learning
  models to develop personalised recommendations... This enhanced personalisation helps customers
  quickly find what they need, benefit from relevant deals, and ultimately drives higher retention
  and order frequency." (TLB-002, page 15)
- FinTech as a retention lever: digital wallets, talabat Postpaid (deferred bill settlement), and
  co-branded credit cards "incentivise continuous platform usage through tailored rewards.
  Combined, these loyalty and financial services lower transaction costs, boost conversion metrics,
  and cultivate a highly retentive customer base" (TLB-002, page 13)
- Egypt-specific retention/loyalty expansion: talabat pro launched in Egypt Feb 2025; talabat
  PostPaid launched in Egypt; co-branded credit card launched with Commercial International Bank
  (CIB), described as Egypt's largest private-sector bank — "Expanded ancillary FinTech services for
  the first time outside of the UAE, starting with Egypt" (TLB-002, pages 14, 19)
- Resilience/retention evidence: following a 5-day administrative closure in Qatar in September
  2025, "our teams executed swiftly, restoring order volumes and demonstrating the strength of our
  operational capabilities, the stickiness of our platform and the loyalty of our customers." (TLB-002, page 9)
- 2026 outlook: >USD 100mn incremental investment (opex + capex) split between scaling talabat mart
  and "strengthening talabat pro as a multi-vertical engagement engine," with incremental investment
  "to focus on enhancing value for both customers and participating vendors." Guidance frames the
  goal as exiting the investment cycle "with a more loyal customer base." (TLB-002, page 10)
- Customer Acquisition and Retention Costs (CARC) internal metric: USD 155mn / 1.6% of GMV in 2025
  (2024: USD 103mn / 1.4% of GMV), combining talabat-funded vouchering (USD 89mn) and customer
  marketing costs (USD 67mn) (TLB-002, page 21)
- Goodwill impairment testing methodology note: "Local management teams use cohort models for
  revenue planning... The key inputs of the cohort models include the customer retention/reorder
  rate, customer activity rate, average order size, and commission rates." (TLB-002, page 100) —
  confirms retention/reorder rate is an internally tracked KPI.
- Investment case: "the one-third of our users engaging across multiple verticals show significantly
  higher order frequency and retention... our talabat pro subscription programme and embedded
  fintech solutions reduce checkout friction, creating a highly retentive environment that amplifies
  each user's lifetime value." (TLB-002, page 7)

## Limitations
- This document uses two different reporting bases that must not be conflated: (1) "pro forma" FY2025
  figures (a 12-month, like-for-like basis assuming the pre-IPO restructuring occurred 1 Jan 2024,
  excluding instashop) used throughout the Strategic Report/Financial Review (pages 17–23), and
  (2) audited "since inception" figures covering the 485-day period from 3 September 2024 to
  31 December 2025, including instashop from 25 February 2025, used in the Financial Statements
  (pages 76–115). The Egypt segment financials (revenue USD 509.94mn, etc.) come from the audited
  485-day basis, NOT the pro forma 12-month FY2025 basis — so they are not directly comparable to
  the pro forma group GMV/revenue figures quoted elsewhere in this report or to TLB-029's FY2024
  pro forma figures.
- There is an apparent internal inconsistency in the GCC/non-GCC GMV growth narrative: the Financial
  Review table (page 18) shows GCC GMV of USD 6,332mn (+20% y/y) and non-GCC GMV of USD 1,096mn
  (+42% y/y), but the accompanying narrative text states GCC GMV "growing... to USD 7.7 billion"
  and non-GCC "57% expansion to USD 1.7 billion" — these narrative figures do not reconcile with the
  table and may reflect a different currency basis, a drafting error, or inclusion of different
  scope; readers should treat the tabulated figures as primary and flag the narrative numbers for
  verification against other corpus documents (e.g., TLB-019/TLB-020 earnings presentations).
  This is noted here as-extracted per the "do not invent facts" rule.
- Despite the Egypt segment being separately disclosed in the audited notes, the pro forma Financial
  Review section (used for headline growth commentary) still discusses Egypt only qualitatively
  (e.g., "growing well over 50%") without a pro forma Egypt GMV or revenue figure — so a full,
  like-for-like Egypt GMV time series is still not available from this document alone.
- Governance-report sections (pages 44–70) are largely procedural/legal (board composition,
  committee charters, remuneration policy mechanics) and contain minimal content relevant to the
  retention strategy business plan; they were reviewed but are not extensively excerpted here.
- Various accounting-policy boilerplate sections (leases, financial instrument classification,
  fair value hierarchy — roughly pages 90–95 of the notes) were reviewed and contain no Egypt- or
  retention-relevant content; omitted from detailed extraction as pure IFRS technical boilerplate.
- Some figures are presented "at a glance" (e.g., subscription revenue USD 74mn on page 11) using a
  slightly different basis than the detailed Financial Review table (subscription fees & other
  income of USD 1,397mn on page 20, which bundles subscription with "other income"); readers should
  not treat these as directly interchangeable.
