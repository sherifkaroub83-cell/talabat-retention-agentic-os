# TLB-026 — Talabat Holding plc International Offering Memorandum

## Title
Talabat Holding plc — International Offering Memorandum (IPO prospectus for the Global Offering of 3,493,236,093 Shares and Admission to the Dubai Financial Market)

## Citation
TLB-026 — Talabat Holding plc, "International Offering Memorandum," 19 November 2024, Input_Data/05_Regulatory_and_IPO/Talabat-Holding-plc_International-Offering-Memorandum.pdf

## Publication Date
19 November 2024

## Publisher
Talabat Holding plc, with Emirates NBD Capital PSC, J.P. Morgan Securities PLC and Morgan Stanley & Co International PLC as Joint Global Coordinators and Joint Bookrunners (together with ADCB, Barclays, EFG-Hermes, FAB, Goldman Sachs Bank Europe SE, ING and UniCredit as Joint Bookrunners) (TLB-026, page 4)

## Executive Summary
This is talabat's IPO prospectus for its listing on the Dubai Financial Market, published 19 November 2024, offering 3,493,236,093 shares (15% of the company) sold entirely by Delivery Hero MENA Holding GmbH at an offer price range of AED 1.50–1.60 (TLB-026, page 4). It contains audited combined and carve-out financial statements for FY2021–FY2023 plus unaudited interim statements through 29 September 2024, full risk factor disclosure, an independent OC&C/Redseer industry report, a detailed business description, and management/shareholder information (TLB-026, page 4, page 19, page 84). talabat is presented as the leading on-demand food ordering, delivery and groceries/convenience-retail marketplace in eight MENA countries (UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Egypt, Oman, Jordan, Iraq), with more than 6 million active customers, 65,000+ Partners and 119,000+ active riders as of September 2024 (TLB-026, page 24). FY2023 GMV was AED 22,263.8 million (USD 6,062 million), revenue AED 7,987.7 million, and the document sets out FY2024/FY2025 growth guidance and medium-term financial targets (TLB-026, page 24, page 139). Egypt is never broken out as a standalone financial segment — it is folded into "Non-GCC" GMV alongside Jordan and Iraq — but the document contains one explicit precise figure: "In 2023, Egypt represented 6% of our total revenue and only 1% of our total Adjusted EBITDA" (TLB-026, page 93). Retention/loyalty mechanics (talabat pro, talabat Rewards, talabat Postpaid, talabat Dineout Deals) are described in unusual depth, including quantified frequency-uplift and retention-lift metrics, and the document explicitly flags that talabat pro and Iraq/Egypt exclusions apply to several of these KPIs, since talabat pro was not yet available in Egypt as of this filing (TLB-026, page 133).

## Key Operational Metrics
- More than 6 million active customers, 65,000+ active Partners (incl. 9,500+ Local Shops), 119,000+ active riders (all September 2024); active-customer CAGR of ~25% from 2.2 million (Jan 2020) to 6 million+ (Sept 2024) (TLB-026, page 24, page 91)
- Average order frequency: 6.5x per customer (Sept 2024) vs 6.0x (Sept 2023); food-only users 3.8 orders/month at AED 194 spend; multi-vertical users 12.8 orders/month at AED 814 spend (Sept 2024) (TLB-026, page 26, page 122)
- Restaurants: 56,300+ across markets (20,000+ in UAE); 4,000+ cloud-kitchen ("Kitchens") partnerships; Local Shops 9,500+, of which 4,000+ in UAE; 700,000+ SKUs across Local Shops (TLB-026, page 27, page 126, page 129)
- tMarts: 127 strategically located stores as of 29 September 2024, spanning Egypt, UAE, Kuwait, Jordan, Qatar, Oman, Bahrain, Iraq; size 200–400 sqm; ~99% seamless order rate; G&R Vertical had 39% penetration of total active customers as of 29 Sept 2024 (TLB-026, page 130-131)
- Order success/experience: ~99% order success rate and average delivery time <30 minutes (Sept 2024); rider average tenure grew from 33 weeks (Jan 2022) to 64 weeks (Sept 2024), a 1.9x increase (TLB-026, page 27, page 138)
- Customer savings: AED 1,565.7m (EUR 391m) aggregate annual savings (29 Sept 2023–29 Sept 2024); ~72% of Partners offered discounts and one-third of orders were discounted (Sept 2024) (TLB-026, page 27)
- Employees: 6,642 total as of 29 Sept 2024 (517 contact centre, 894 fleet management, 627 tPatrollers/riders, 1,232 sales, 481 product & tech, 1,522 support functions, 1,369 tMart & Kitchens); ~90 nationalities; 94% high-performer retention and 13% attrition (9M2024) (TLB-026, page 140)
- Technology: 480+ product/design/engineering/data technologists across Dubai and Cairo tech-hubs; ~13 terabytes of data generated daily (as of 29 Sept 2024) (TLB-026, page 118, page 131)
- Properties: 2 owned properties (land in Jordan) and 211 leased properties (27 Kitchens, 127 tMart stores, 57 offices/hubs/warehouses), including 56 properties in Egypt (43 tMart stores + 13 offices/warehouses) (TLB-026, page 145)
- Multi-vertical customers: 28% of customers were multi-vertical (ordered from both Food and G&R Verticals) for December 2023, a 38% CAGR from Dec 2019–Dec 2023 (TLB-026, page 128)

## Countries Mentioned
The eight "talabat countries": UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Egypt, Oman, Jordan, Iraq — five GCC (UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman) and three Non-GCC (Jordan, Egypt, Iraq) (TLB-026, page 108). Also referenced: Delivery Hero's ~70-country global footprint across Asia, MENA, Europe and Americas (TLB-026, page 24); comparator "Western Countries" (USA, Canada, and 15 Western European countries) used throughout the Industry Overview (TLB-026, page 108-109); countries named in the MENA political-unrest risk factor — Bahrain, Egypt, Iran, Iraq, Libya, Syria, Tunisia, Yemen (TLB-026, page 76); Germany (Delivery Hero's Frankfurt listing, German law governing several agreements) (TLB-026, page 124).

## Egypt Mentions
- Egypt listed among the eight countries of talabat's MENA operations (TLB-026, page 24, page 108, page 116).
- Egypt is one of three "Non-GCC countries" (with Jordan and Iraq); Non-GCC GMV is defined as GMV from Egypt, Jordan and Iraq combined — no country-level GMV split within Non-GCC (TLB-026, page 41, page 108).
- "In 2023, Egypt represented 6% of our total revenue and only 1% of our total Adjusted EBITDA" — the single most precise Egypt-specific financial datapoint in the document (TLB-026, page 93).
- Egypt is the one talabat country whose currency (Egyptian Pound) is *not* pegged to the US Dollar — it operates a managed float; seven of the eight talabat countries maintain a USD peg (TLB-026, page 109).
- Currency risk: "a devaluation of local currencies against the US Dollar... as has been the case in Egypt... has led to higher costs for imported goods and services, reducing our profit margins" (TLB-026, page 75). The Egyptian Pound was further devalued in March 2024; prior to that there was a severe USD shortage in the market causing fund-transfer restrictions; earnings in Egypt "may be difficult to repatriate" (TLB-026, page 74).
- FY2023 vs FY2022 foreign exchange loss decrease "primarily attributable to the devaluation of the Egyptian Pound"; the 9M2024 foreign exchange loss increase (AED 213.3m vs AED 68.8m) was "primarily attributable to an unrealised non-cash loss related to the revaluation of the parent loan to our Egyptian subsidiary as a result of the devaluation of the Egyptian Pound" (TLB-026, page 97, page 99).
- Adjusted Net Profit definition explicitly excludes "non-cash unrealised foreign exchange loss from shareholder loan liability in Delivery Hero Egypt SAE" (TLB-026, page 19, page 42).
- Cash-heavy market risk: Egypt named alongside Iraq and Jordan as markets where "cash transactions are more prevalent and banking systems may be less robust," increasing exposure to theft, counterfeit currency, and cash-reconciliation risk (TLB-026, page 60).
- Delivery/mapping risk: "not every customer has an individual, easily identifiable address... this is also prevalent in areas where new roads and communities are constantly being built... (e.g., Egypt and UAE)" (TLB-026, page 61).
- Third-party logistics concentration: in Bahrain, Qatar, Oman, Iraq and Egypt, "the top seven third-party logistics providers represent approximately 50% of the rider fleet" in their respective markets (TLB-026, page 51).
- Labour disruption: talabat "experienced rider disruptions on a smaller scale" in Jordan, Egypt, Oman and Iraq (following a larger May 2022 Dubai rider strike) (TLB-026, page 52).
- Political-unrest risk factor lists Egypt among MENA countries that have experienced political unrest (with Bahrain, Iran, Iraq, Libya, Syria, Tunisia, Yemen) (TLB-026, page 76).
- Internet/mobile infrastructure risk: "difficulties of developing mobile infrastructure and, at times, electricity outages or unreliable internet networks in some of the markets in which we operate, such as Egypt, may limit [growth]" (TLB-026, page 50).
- talabat pro is available in all markets **except Egypt and Iraq**, as of the date of this Offering Memorandum (19 November 2024) (TLB-026, page 133).
- History: talabat expanded to Jordan and Egypt in 2017 (launch of own-delivery service); operated in Egypt under the "Otlob" brand, rebranded to talabat in September 2020 (TLB-026, page 124, page 140).
- talabat holds the "Otlob" trademark in Egypt (used historically) and the "Talabat" trademark in Egypt among other jurisdictions (TLB-026, page 140).
- talabat operates the Delivery Hero-owned "Everyday Roastery" brand in Egypt and the UAE (TLB-026, page 140).
- InstaShop (being acquired by the Group) "primarily operates in the groceries and retail space in the UAE and Egypt" (TLB-026, page 31).
- 80% of customers in Kuwait, UAE, Egypt, Qatar and Bahrain ranked talabat as their #1 preferred platform (as of 29 Sept 2024 survey) (TLB-026, page 27, page 119).
- Egypt included among the eight countries surveyed by OC&C/Redseer for consumer preference, awareness, usage and NPS benchmarking (TLB-026, page 136-137).
- Rider training: "tcamp" (online training app) deployed in Egypt, UAE, Bahrain, Kuwait and Oman; separate rider survey/interviews conducted in UAE, Kuwait, Qatar and Egypt (TLB-026, page 138-139).
- tMarts operate in Egypt among the eight markets (127 tMarts total); the Group's property table shows 56 leased properties in Egypt (43 tMart stores, 13 offices/hubs/warehouses) — the single largest non-UAE property footprint (TLB-026, page 130, page 145).
- talabat holds insurance policies covering Egypt among all eight jurisdictions of operation (TLB-026, page 144).
- The Group's Pre-IPO Reorganisation involved the transfer of Delivery Hero's 100% indirect shareholding in Delivery Hero Egypt SAE into the Company's "Extended Perimeter Group" (30 September 2024) (TLB-026, page 18, page 125-126).
- "talabat Egypt Services Agreements" — services agreements between Delivery Hero and Delivery Hero Egypt SAE (effective 1 June 2020) and Delivery Hero Dmart Egypt LLC (effective 1 April 2024), on substantially similar terms to the UAE Services Agreement, governed by the laws of Egypt (TLB-026, page 158).

## Retention-Relevant Information
- **The "talabat flywheel"**: network effects across customers, Partners and riders that drive growth, service quality and platform loyalty; average order frequency 6.5x (Sept 2024) vs 6.0x (Sept 2023) (TLB-026, page 118).
- **Customer cohort analysis**: talabat tracks customers by first-order-year cohort; each cohort has consistently increased spend in subsequent years; GMV for cohorts acquired since 2019 grew ~4x over 5 years; projected full-year-2024 multiples: 3.5x since 2019, 3.1x since 2020, 2.9x since 2021, 2.7x since 2022, 2.0x since 2023 (TLB-026, page 120).
- **talabat pro** (subscription/loyalty programme, launched March 2022 in UAE): free delivery (minimum basket) + exclusive deals for a monthly/annual fee; available in all markets **except Egypt and Iraq** as of filing date; only 8.3% of customers were subscribed as of 29 Sept 2024, framed as "substantial room for growth"; UAE subscriber CAGR of 130% (Sept 2022–Sept 2024); >20% order-frequency uplift among subscribers (July 2024); retention is explicitly measured — "percentage of talabat pro customers that are retained on talabat 12 months after joining talabat pro, relative to lookalike customers" (TLB-026, page 30, page 122, page 133-134).
- **talabat Rewards** (points-based loyalty programme launched 2020): no subscription/enrolment required; designed "to increase customer spend and retention"; 18% adoption rate and >15% order-frequency uplift among first-time redeemers (as of 29 Sept 2024); features gamification (raffle tickets/prizes) and points redeemable within 60 days (TLB-026, page 134-135).
- **talabat Dineout Deals** (launched May 2024, UAE only): no-subscription dine-out discount programme that also cross-sells/strengthens talabat pro value; framed as driving "increased frequency of visits to Restaurants" (TLB-026, page 135).
- **talabat Postpaid** (FinTech/loyalty adjacent, UAE only): allows billing up to 30 days after purchase; led to a 14% increase in order frequency and reduced order cyclicality (Nov 2021–Jan 2024 measurement); only 2.7% of customers used it as of 29 Sept 2024; CAGR of Postpaid users was 228% (Sept 2022–Sept 2024) (TLB-026, page 30, page 122).
- **Co-branded cards** (ADCB partnership, UAE, since August 2023): welcome-bonus and cashback mechanics designed to digitally acquire and lock in an engaged, digitally-savvy customer base (TLB-026, page 136).
- **Multi-vertical strategy as a retention lever**: multi-vertical (Food + Groceries & Retail) customers order 12.8x/month at AED 814 spend vs. 3.8x/month at AED 194 for food-only users — the single starkest frequency/spend uplift metric in the document (TLB-026, page 122).
- **Personalisation & marketing engine**: "automated marketing engine learns based on previous customer behaviour and suggests the next best action or reactivation flows for those customers that might have reduced their ordering frequency" (TLB-026, page 137). Machine-learning models are used to "introduce and cross-sell verticals, as well as initiatives such as talabat pro and talabat Rewards, at the right time for our customers" (TLB-026, page 121); ad-personalisation features estimated to have driven ~USD 14 million incremental Adjusted EBITDA since January 2023 (TLB-026, page 133).
- **Risk-side retention signals**: "If we fail to retain our existing customers and Partners... our marketing spend may increase and our business as well as our results may be negatively affected" — the Company explicitly frames new-customer acquisition as substantially organic (word-of-mouth, social, non-paid referral) but still incurs ongoing acquisition costs (TLB-026, page 57-58). Delivery/experience failures are explicitly tied to "customer retention" risk (TLB-026, page 61).
- **GEMs** (Guaranteed Engagement Metrics, an AdTech product): "recommended for Restaurant with low/declining customer acquisition or retention rates" — a Partner-side retention/reactivation tool (TLB-026, page 138).
- **Data-driven personalised recommendations require at least 6 orders per customer** to activate, tying data volume directly to retention tooling maturity (TLB-026, page 131).

## Limitations
- Egypt is never reported as a standalone financial or operational segment anywhere in this document (P&L, balance sheet, KPI tables, or the audited financial-statement notes' geographic disclosure), which splits revenue only into "GCC" and "Non-GCC" (Egypt + Jordan + Iraq combined). The single explicit Egypt-only figure is the qualitative "6% of revenue / 1% of Adjusted EBITDA" statement for FY2023 (page 93) — no Egypt GMV, active-customer, order, or margin figures are disclosed anywhere.
- As of this filing (19 November 2024), talabat pro was not yet available in Egypt, so essentially all quantified subscription-retention KPIs (adoption rate, frequency uplift, 12-month retention lift, subscriber CAGR) explicitly exclude Egypt. talabat Dineout Deals and Postpaid were UAE-only at the time. Readers should not extrapolate GCC/UAE loyalty-programme performance to Egypt.
- Financial statements cover FY2021–FY2023 (audited) and the 9-month period through 29 September 2024 (unaudited/reviewed only); this is a pre-IPO snapshot and does not include any results after Q3 2024.
- Market/industry sizing data (TAM, penetration rates, competitive positioning) is sourced from a single commissioned third-party report (OC&C Strategy Consultants and Redseer Consulting, research conducted August–September 2024) and is explicitly flagged by the Company as forward-looking, estimate-based, and not independently verified.
- Medium-term financial targets (page 139) are explicitly stated not to be a profit forecast; achievement depends on numerous stated risk factors.
- The document is written for a securities-offering audience (investors, regulators) and is heavily larded with legal/regulatory boilerplate (transfer restrictions, taxation, sale-of-shares mechanics) not relevant to a retention strategy; this note deliberately does not extract that content.
- InstaShop-related figures should not be confused across metrics: the USD 31.9 million figure is the intra-group transfer/capital price for the acquisition, not InstaShop's GMV (~USD 487 million in 2023). This distinction is directly relevant to the corpus verification flag on InstaShop valuation figures.
