## Executive Summary
This is talabat Holding PLC's second full audited annual financial statement, covering the cumulative period from inception (3 September 2024) through 31 December 2025 (TLB-008, page 1). For calendar year 2025 (per the Directors' Report), the Group delivered GMV of USD 9.4 billion (+27% YoY), revenue of USD 3.9 billion (+31%), Adjusted EBITDA of ~USD 615 million (+24%, 6.5% of GMV), and net income of USD 464 million (+34%, 4.9% of GMV; USD 451 million / 4.8% of GMV on a like-for-like basis) (TLB-008, page 3). For the full cumulative period since inception through 31 December 2025, audited revenue was USD 4,651,861,456 and net profit was USD 605,594,580 (TLB-008, pages 3, 10). Total assets at 31 December 2025 were USD 1,620,397,597 against total equity of USD 699,928,012 (TLB-008, page 9). The single most significant change versus TLB-004 (FY2024) is in segment reporting: Egypt now individually meets the IFRS 8 quantitative thresholds and is disclosed as its own reportable segment, separate from both the GCC and the residual "Non-GCC" (Jordan, Iraq) grouping (TLB-008, page 39). The segment note discloses, for the period since inception through 31 December 2025, Egypt external revenue of USD 509,936,177, gross profit of USD 124,995,968, and net profit of USD 44,354,242 — making Egypt the Group's third-largest country by revenue behind the UAE (USD 1,680,452,563) and Kuwait (USD 1,175,785,453), and ahead of Qatar (USD 462,169,888) (TLB-008, page 61). Egypt is mentioned 14 times across the document, spanning the MENA-footprint description, the subsidiary/entity list (Delivery Hero Egypt SAE, Delivery Hero Dmart Egypt LLC, InstaShop LLC), the reportable-segment note and table, the geographic revenue table, currency risk disclosures (Egyptian Pound), and the InstaShop common-control acquisition note.

## Retention-Relevant Information
- Subscription fees are disclosed as a distinct revenue line: USD 83,790,496 for the period since inception through 31 December 2025 — up from USD 9,728,074 in the FY2024 audited statement (TLB-004), an increase of roughly 8.6x over the cumulative period, though the two figures cover different-length periods (120 days vs. ~16 months) so are not directly comparable as a growth rate (TLB-008, page 52).
- Revenue-recognition policy note (unchanged from FY2024): "For subscription programs offered to orderers and restaurants, the Group acts as principal. Revenue from subscription fees is recognised on a straight-line basis over the period of the subscription." (TLB-008, page 22, per Note 3.3 policy language reiterated in this statement)
- Note 6 (Intangible assets and goodwill) impairment-testing methodology again confirms: "The key inputs of the cohort models include the customer retention/reorder rate, customer activity rate, average order size, and commission rates" — used in the Talabat UAE and Talabat Kuwait goodwill valuation models (TLB-008, page 45).
- Egypt becoming a standalone reportable segment (rather than being bundled into "Non-GCC") is itself retention-strategy-relevant: it signals Egypt has grown large enough (revenue, assets, or profit each individually exceeding 10% of Group totals per IFRS 8.13) to warrant separate performance review by the CODM, implying growing strategic and analytical visibility into Egypt-specific customer/revenue dynamics going forward (TLB-008, page 39).
- No further Egypt-specific retention, loyalty-program, or churn commentary is present; this remains a statutory financial statement without MD&A-style narrative.

## Limitations
- Reported figures on the primary statements (P&L, cash flow) are cumulative "since inception" (3 September 2024 through 31 December 2025, ~16 months), not a clean FY2025 calendar-year figure; only the Directors' Report narrative (page 3) restates headline metrics (GMV, revenue, EBITDA, net income) on a calendar-year 2025 basis, and these narrative figures are not separately reconciled to the audited notes within this document.
- Even though Egypt is now a reportable segment, the disclosure is limited to revenue, gross profit, net profit/(loss), delivery cost, and depreciation/amortization for the cumulative since-inception period (page 61) — there is no Egypt-specific GMV, order volume, active-customer count, or EBITDA margin, and no quarter-by-quarter or calendar-year-only Egypt breakout.
- No operational KPIs (orders, active customers, riders, restaurant partner counts) anywhere in the document — purely a statutory IFRS financial statement.
- No narrative discussion of competitive dynamics or market risk beyond standard FX/interest-rate financial-instrument risk disclosures.
- Non-current assets by geography table only itemizes UAE explicitly, folding Egypt into "Others" (page 61), so Egypt's balance-sheet/asset base cannot be isolated from this document.
