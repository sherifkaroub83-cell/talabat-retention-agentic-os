# Competitors — Consolidated Entity List

Deduplicated from all 29 documents' raw facts fragments. Talabat's own disclosures are notably sparse on named competitors — most references are generic category descriptions rather than named rivals.

## Named competitors

Only one document in the entire corpus names specific competitor brands: the **International Offering Memorandum (TLB-026, page 146)**, which names **Deliveroo, Careem, noon, Jahez, and Snoonu** as direct competitors.

A China-market benchmark reference in the Capital Markets Day deck (TLB-015) names **Meituan**, but only as an illustrative international comparison, not a MENA competitor.

**Zomato** (UAE assets) is named in TLB-023 and TLB-026 in the context of a strategic alliance/asset acquisition by talabat in the UAE — not as an active competitor.

## Generic competitive framing (all other documents)

Every other document that discusses competition does so generically, without naming rivals:
- "Offline restaurants and shops offering delivery," "restaurants/retailers building their own online ordering capability" (TLB-001)
- "Technology giants and well-funded entrants" (TLB-001)
- "Integrated e-commerce companies, quick commerce providers and 'SuperApps'" (TLB-001)
- "Traditional offline [alternatives]" (TLB-002)
- "Next largest online peer platform" / "regional peers" per OC&C/Redseer third-party analysis (TLB-015, TLB-023)

## Documents with no competitor content

TLB-004, TLB-008 (financial statements — no competitive discussion), TLB-027, TLB-028 (consensus reports — not covered).

## Implication for the capstone

The corpus itself does not provide a rich named-competitor dataset for Egypt specifically. The project's separate secondary-source corpus (`Input_Data/03_Competitors/2026-07-20_egypt_delivery_competitive_landscape.md`) — covering Rabbit, Breadfast, elmenus, Akelni/Mrsool, Roboost — remains the primary source for Egypt-specific competitive intelligence and should be cross-referenced alongside this primary-source corpus.
