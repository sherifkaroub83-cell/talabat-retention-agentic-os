# Customer Churn

## Overview
There is no dedicated "churn" Facts file in the vault, and talabat's disclosures almost never use the word "churn" directly — the corpus discusses churn almost exclusively as retention's mirror image. The clearest direct statement is the 2026 strategic objective to "retain high and medium value customers against partial or complete churn to competition" (TLB-002, page 14), which explicitly names competitive churn (customers switching to rivals) as the threat being managed, as distinct from customers simply lapsing. A second, indirect churn signal is operational rather than customer-level: the September 2025 five-day administrative closure in Qatar, after which talabat highlighted the swift recovery of order volumes as evidence against churn — "restoring order volumes and demonstrating the strength of our operational capabilities, the stickiness of our platform and the loyalty of our customers" (TLB-002, page 9).

## Why It Matters
Churn is the inverse problem the capstone's AI-driven retention strategy exists to solve, but the vault's evidence for it is thin and almost entirely inferential — this note should be read as a synthesis under acknowledged data-gap conditions, not as a churn-metrics summary. The one explicit, named churn risk in the corpus — "partial or complete churn to competition" — is precisely the risk an Egypt market entering a newly competitive phase (see [[Competition]], [[Egypt]]) needs to plan against, which is directly relevant to justifying AI-driven, targeted (rather than blanket) retention spend.

## Open Questions
- The vault has no explicit churn rate, churn definition, or churn cohort analysis for talabat overall or for Egypt specifically — every "churn" reference in the corpus is qualitative or inferred from a retention statement.
- It is not clear from the vault whether talabat distinguishes "voluntary churn to a named competitor" from "involuntary" or "dormant" churn (e.g., customers who simply stop ordering without switching platforms) — the 2026 objective language ("partial or complete churn to competition") suggests the former is the primary concern, but no supporting data breaks this down.
- No Egypt-specific churn or reorder-rate figure exists in the corpus, despite Egypt being flagged elsewhere in the vault as an increasingly competitive market (see [[Competition]], [[Egypt]]).
- The goodwill-impairment cohort models (TLB-004, TLB-008) reference a "customer retention/reorder rate" input but do not disclose its value — this is a genuine gap between what talabat internally models and what is publicly disclosed.

## Business Implications
- Because the vault provides no churn baseline for Egypt, the capstone's AI-driven retention strategy cannot cite a current Egypt churn rate and should instead frame its target as proactive, evidence-based risk mitigation against the named "churn to competition" threat, using talabat's own group-wide retention levers (subscription, rewards, personalization) as the toolkit.
- The Qatar closure recovery narrative supports an implication that platform stickiness (multi-vertical usage, habitual ordering) is itself a churn-resistance mechanism — an argument the capstone can extend to Egypt via [[Customer Journey]] and multi-vertical adoption.
- Given the explicit data gap, any churn-rate figures used illustratively in the business plan must be treated as synthetic/hypothetical and clearly labeled as such, per the project's standing instruction on synthetic data.
