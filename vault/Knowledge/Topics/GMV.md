# GMV

## Overview
Gross Merchandise Value is talabat's headline scale metric — the total value of goods and services transacted on the platform, excluding subscription fees and rider tips (TLB-001, page 27). It is reported quarterly by geography (GCC vs. non-GCC/Egypt) and by vertical (Food vs. Groceries & Retail), and every set of investor materials in the corpus frames guidance, beats, and strategy around it. GMV grew from USD 6,062mn (FY2023) to USD 7,428mn (FY2024, +23% YoY) to USD 9,421–9,512mn (FY2025, +27–28% YoY), with FY2026 guidance stepping down to 11–14% cFX growth as the base matures. See [[GMV]] (Facts) for the complete, per-document cited series.

## Why It Matters
GMV is the top of talabat's growth funnel and the base against which every other metric in this note-set is indexed — take rate (Revenue/GMV), profitability margins (EBITDA/GMV, Net Income/GMV), and segment weight are all expressed as a share of it. For the retention thesis, the corpus explicitly attributes 2024 GMV growth to a 25% expansion in monthly active users **and** an 8% uplift in order frequency (TLB-001, page 26) — meaning retention-driven frequency gains, not just new-customer acquisition, are already a named, quantified component of GMV growth, and Egypt's rapid GMV share gain gives that dynamic a live, high-growth market to compound in.

## Open Questions
- The vault does not disaggregate Egypt-specific GMV as a standalone number pre-FY2025 (it is bundled into non-GCC with Jordan and Iraq) — how much of the non-GCC GMV acceleration seen through 2023–2024 in TLB-001/TLB-026 was actually Egypt versus Jordan/Iraq is not answerable from the corpus.
- TLB-002's own GMV tables show an internal reconciliation gap between the segment table (GCC USD 6,332mn / non-GCC USD 1,096mn) and its narrative text (GCC USD 7.7bn / non-GCC USD 1.7bn) for what appears to be the same FY2025 period — the vault flags this as unreconciled rather than resolving it.
- No document in the corpus gives an Egypt-only GMV figure in dollar terms, even after Egypt became a standalone P&L segment — segment disclosure covers revenue/profit, not GMV, per [[Countries]].

## Business Implications
- Because order frequency (not just customer acquisition) is a named, quantified GMV driver, an AI-driven retention strategy for Egypt can credibly target GMV uplift through frequency mechanisms already proven elsewhere in the group (talabat pro, rewards) rather than needing to invent a new growth lever.
- Egypt's outsized non-GCC GMV growth rates suggest the market is in a high-elasticity phase where retention interventions (subscription, personalization) may have larger marginal GMV impact than in the more saturated GCC core.
- The shift toward Groceries & Retail's rising GMV share is relevant to retention strategy design: multi-vertical engagement is linked elsewhere in the vault to higher order frequency, so retention tactics that cross-sell Egypt customers into G&R could compound GMV impact beyond food-only retention.
