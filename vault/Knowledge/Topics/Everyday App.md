# Everyday App

## Overview
"Everyday App" is talabat's own label for the larger of the two components of its 2026 Board-approved
investment programme — the ~USD 120 million allocated to scaling talabat mart dark stores, deepening
the talabat pro loyalty programme, and building new retail/verticals offerings, as distinct from the
smaller ~USD 55 million "Food leadership" component (TLB-020, page 12, page 16; TLB-014, page 8, page
19; TLB-011, page 2). Management frames the underlying strategic logic explicitly: "As customer
behaviour becomes increasingly multi-vertical, improving frequency, basket size, and retention position
talabat to scale its Everyday App across our markets" (TLB-020, page 16; TLB-014, page 19). The name
formalizes a narrative arc talabat had already been building — from "Delivering food to your doorstep"
(2016) to "Delivering food and a lot more" (2021) to "It's not just talabat app, it's your app" (2025)
(TLB-016, page 7) — into a funded, named 2026 investment category with a specific dollar figure attached.

## Why It Matters
Everyday App is literally one half of the decision this capstone's plan must resolve: how talabat's USD
175 million 2026 programme should be allocated between it and Food leadership, and — within it — across
markets and initiative categories (talabat mart, talabat pro, new verticals). The evidence base for
Everyday App is exactly the mechanism (multi-vertical GMV share, talabat pro GMV share, order frequency)
that the plan's value driver tree must trace from investment to GMV, revenue, and EBITDA. Because
Everyday App already receives the larger half of the 2026 budget (~120m vs ~55m), understanding what it
funds and what evidence supports its return is the single most consequential input to the allocation
recommendation.

## Open Questions
- No source discloses a country-level or market-tier split of the ~USD 120mn Everyday App allocation — it is a Group-wide figure only.
- The corpus does not state an expected ROI, payback period, or target KPI threshold for the Everyday App programme as a whole; TLB-020's own language ("investments rather than costs... expected to offset the margin impact over time," page 16) is qualitative.
- It is unclear from the corpus whether "Everyday App" is a genuinely new strategic category invented for 2026, or simply a new funding label attached to initiatives (talabat mart scaling, talabat pro deepening) that were already underway in 2024-2025 under other names — the vault does not resolve this.
- No source specifies how the ~USD 75mn opex / ~USD 45mn capex split within Everyday App (TLB-014, page 19; TLB-020, page 12) is itself divided across talabat mart, talabat pro, and new verticals individually.

## Business Implications
- Because Everyday App already receives roughly 2.2x the Food-leadership allocation (~120m vs ~55m), any recommendation to shift capital between the two components should explicitly engage with this existing weighting, not treat the split as unset.
- The multi-vertical and talabat-pro GMV-share trends (both currently well above 2024 levels) are the strongest disclosed evidence that Everyday App capital is coinciding with measurable behaviour change, though the corpus cannot isolate investment-caused uplift from other concurrent drivers (seasonality, macro tailwinds cited in TLB-011/TLB-014 for Q1 2026).
- Because talabat mart carries a lower take rate/margin profile than Food (see [[Grocery and Retail]]), a recommendation to increase Everyday App's grocery/retail share should account for the disclosed EBITDA-margin drag, not treat all Everyday App GMV growth as equally profitable.
- Given the absence of a market-level split, any country-by-country Everyday App allocation the business plan proposes must be presented as a staged, ranged assumption grounded in Group evidence — not as a disclosed or derivable fact.
