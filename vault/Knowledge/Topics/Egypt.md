# Egypt

## Overview
Egypt is one of talabat's 8 operating markets and, within the corpus's own timeline, the market whose financial visibility has changed the most. For most of the corpus's history Egypt had no standalone financial disclosure at all — it was folded into a "non-GCC" segment together with Jordan and Iraq, with the only pre-2025 Egypt-specific figure being the IPO prospectus's disclosure that Egypt was 6% of 2023 revenue but just 1% of Adjusted EBITDA (TLB-026, page 27ish per [[Countries|Entities/Countries]]). Starting with the FY2025 Annual Report and Q4/FY2025 audited financials, Egypt independently met the IFRS 8 quantitative thresholds and became its own standalone reportable segment, distinct from GCC and the residual Jordan+Iraq "Non-GCC" bucket — see [[Countries|Entities/Countries]] for the full disclosure history and figures. talabat pro, the company's core subscription loyalty programme, launched in Egypt in February 2025 — one of the last major markets to receive it (only Iraq followed, in September 2025).

## Why It Matters
Egypt is the direct subject of this capstone's AI-driven retention strategy, so this topic is the single most load-bearing note in the vault. The corpus's own narrative arc — an undisclosed sub-segment becoming a standalone, profitable, fast-growing segment with talabat pro live for roughly a year by the time of the FY2025 results — is described in [[Relationship_Map]] (section 7) as "the central empirical basis for the capstone's retention-strategy recommendation": Egypt has just crossed the threshold where the corpus's own retention mechanisms (talabat pro frequency uplift, AI personalization, rewards) have measurable room to compound. Egypt becoming the Group's largest user base and 3rd-largest market by GMV, while still a young talabat pro market, is precisely the "large base, early-stage loyalty penetration" profile that makes a retention-focused (rather than pure-acquisition) strategy the higher-leverage move.

## Open Questions
- No fact in the corpus discloses an Egypt-specific talabat pro adoption rate, frequency uplift, or retention uplift — all uplift metrics (28% frequency, 26% retention, etc.) are Group-level or explicitly exclude Egypt/Iraq at the time they were measured (e.g. TLB-012, TLB-013, TLB-016 explicitly exclude Egypt from the "2.1x adoption growth" figure because Egypt's programme wasn't live yet).
- The corpus contains two different relative category-share figures for Egypt that don't obviously reconcile: TLB-001 (page 5) and TLB-002 (page 5) both state Egypt's food-service category share is "10x+" versus the next-closest peer, while TLB-014 (page 4) states Egypt's food category share was "1x+ (IPO)" rising to "4x+ (YE'25)" — these appear to be different measurement methodologies or time bases, but the vault does not explain the discrepancy.
- No fact discloses an Egypt-specific Partner count, restaurant count, or rider count broken out from platform-wide totals — Egypt's supply-side density cannot be assessed from this corpus alone.
- Egypt-specific named competitors are not covered by this primary-source corpus at all (see [[Competition]] Open Questions) — a real gap for a capstone whose deliverable is an Egypt-specific strategy.
- The corpus does not disclose an Egypt-specific CAC, CARC, or customer lifetime value figure — all such metrics are Group-level.

## Business Implications
- Egypt's status as the Group's largest user base but a very young talabat pro market (live ~1 year at FY2025 reporting) is the strongest evidence-based case in the vault for prioritizing loyalty/subscription-driven retention over further acquisition spend in the Egypt strategy.
- The swing from near-breakeven (Q1 2025 profit before tax USD 0.5m) to solidly profitable (Q1 2026 USD 9.4m) in one year, per [[Countries|Entities/Countries]], suggests Egypt has reached a scale where retention investment (which compounds LTV) is now economically justified in a way it may not have been pre-2025.
- Because no Egypt-specific talabat pro uplift figure exists, any retention-strategy financial modeling for Egypt must explicitly label Group-level uplift figures (28% frequency, 26% retention) as proxies, not confirmed Egypt figures — a labeling discipline the business plan should carry through.
- The unresolved category-share discrepancy (10x+ vs. 1x+→4x+) means the business plan should cite a single source consistently for Egypt's competitive position rather than blending the two figures.
