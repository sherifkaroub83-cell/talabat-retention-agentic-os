# Riders

## Overview
Riders are the fulfillment leg of talabat's three-sided marketplace, growing from >123,000 Active Riders (December 2024) to 168,000 (March 2026) — a 64% CAGR from ~13,000 in Dec-2019 (TLB-001, page 4; TLB-020, page 8; TLB-015, page 17, 55). talabat relies substantially on third-party logistics (3PL) providers and freelance riders rather than direct employment — ~90-95% of riders operate via 3PL or freelance arrangements across different corpus documents, flagged consistently as an operational risk around labour compliance and service-quality control (TLB-001, page 34, 47; TLB-026, page 138). Riders are supported by Delivery-Hero-licensed technology (dispatch algorithms, real-time GPS tracking, a rider staffing algorithm) and are measured on a Rider Safety Score that stood at 94-95% through 2024-2025 ([[Logistics|Facts/Logistics]]).

## Why It Matters
Rider capacity and quality directly gate delivery speed and reliability, which are foundational to the customer experience talabat pro and AI personalization are trying to retain — a retention strategy cannot compound if fulfillment quality lags. The corpus explicitly ties rider-network expansion to customer experience ("reinforcing delivery quality and strengthening the customer experience," TLB-014, page 14), and documents that riders earn 1.2x the daily income of comparable-platform riders (a supply-side retention signal) and keep 100% of over USD 21.5 million in 2025 tips — relevant context for the AI-driven retention business plan if rider-side stability is treated as an input to customer-side service quality.

## Open Questions
- No Egypt-specific Active Rider count is disclosed in the corpus; rider figures are reported only at the 8-country platform level.
- The vault does not evidence a direct causal link between rider safety/satisfaction metrics and customer retention or churn — the connection is plausible but not sourced.
- Rider attrition/turnover rate is not disclosed (only average tenure, which grew from 33 to 64 weeks platform-wide, TLB-026, page 138); no comparison of Egypt rider tenure or working conditions to other markets exists in the corpus.

## Business Implications
- Because rider capacity is the physical constraint on delivery-time reliability, any AI-driven retention strategy premised on service-quality consistency should treat rider-network health (safety score, tenure, staffing-algorithm accuracy) as a dependency, not an independent lever.
- The heavy reliance on 3PL/freelance riders (flagged by talabat itself as a compliance/quality-control risk) is a relevant risk factor to name in the business plan's risk section when proposing any retention mechanism that assumes consistent delivery experience.
- Egypt's 200-EV pilot (end-2025, TLB-002) is the only rider-related Egypt-specific data point in the corpus and is too narrow to support a broader claim about Egypt's rider network — it should be cited narrowly, not extrapolated into a general Egypt fleet-quality claim.
