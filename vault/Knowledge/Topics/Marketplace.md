# Marketplace

## Overview
talabat's core business is a three-sided marketplace connecting Customers, Restaurants/Local Shops ("Partners"), and Riders, generating network effects the company itself labels the "talabat flywheel" ([[Marketplace|Facts/Marketplace]], TLB-001, page 4, page 15). talabat operates this marketplace as an agent — earning commission fees rather than owning inventory — across all 8 MENA countries, holding the #1 category position in every one, with relative category share ranging 1x+ to 10x+ versus the next-largest platform (TLB-014, page 4). The Partner base has scaled from ~15,000 (Jan 2019) to 93,000+ Active Partners (Mar 2026), with penetration still only ~20-25% in foodservice and ~1-2% in grocery/retail — headroom the corpus repeatedly flags (TLB-001, page 21).

## Why It Matters
The marketplace is the demand-and-supply substrate every retention lever operates on: talabat pro's frequency uplift, AI personalization, and advertising all monetize or optimize activity that happens inside this three-sided structure. For talabat Egypt specifically, the marketplace's category-leadership and network-effect dynamics are the precondition for any retention strategy — a retention play only compounds if the underlying flywheel (more customers → more Partner selection → more customers) is healthy in-market, which the corpus confirms via Egypt's rapid segment growth (see [[Egypt]]).

## Open Questions
- The vault does not disclose an Egypt-specific Partner or restaurant count broken out from the platform-wide totals — Egypt marketplace density (Partners per capita, category share vs. local competitors) is not directly evidenced.
- No fact in the corpus quantifies how marketplace category share specifically translates into customer retention or switching costs in Egypt.

## Business Implications
- Any Egypt retention strategy inherits the marketplace's underlying scale advantage (category leadership, broad Partner selection) as a starting asset, per [[Marketplace|Facts/Marketplace]].
- The large gap between foodservice penetration (~20-25%) and grocery/retail penetration (~1-2%) suggests retention gains may come disproportionately from cross-selling existing Food customers into G&R/Q-Commerce rather than only acquiring new customers.
- Because talabat earns commission (not full order value) on marketplace transactions, retention economics should be modeled off net take-rate impact, not gross GMV, when tied to the AI-driven retention business case.
