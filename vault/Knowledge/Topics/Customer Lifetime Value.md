# Customer Lifetime Value

## Overview
talabat does not disclose a formal "CLV" or "LTV" metric by that name, but the concept runs through the corpus via cohort economics: GMV per average customer grows roughly 4x within five years of acquisition (2019 cohort basis, TLB-015), and cumulative orders per cohort keep rising through month 60 across the 2019–2023 cohorts. The IPO prospectus quantifies this more precisely with projected FY2024 spend multiples by acquisition-year cohort (3.5x since 2019 down to 2.0x since 2023, TLB-026, page 120). talabat pro adds a second, causally-measured LTV lever: subscribers show a 136% gross-profit-per-customer uplift in the 30 days following subscription versus the 30 days before (TLB-015, page 78, 104) — the corpus's clearest direct profitability-per-customer figure. The strategic pillar name itself — "turbo-charging customer loyalty," targeting "order frequency, lifetime value and user stickiness" (TLB-001, page 21) — confirms LTV is an explicit internal target, even without a disclosed dollar figure.

## Why It Matters
LTV is the financial justification for retention spend: if the corpus shows cohorts becoming worth ~4x more within five years and talabat pro subscribers becoming worth 136% more within 30 days, that is the evidence base an Egypt-focused AI retention strategy needs to build a value-driver-tree business case (per the project's McKinsey-standard requirement) for reinvesting in personalization and loyalty rather than one-off acquisition. Because Egypt's customer base is newly disclosed as its own segment (TLB-008) with talabat pro live for only about a year, Egypt-specific cohort or LTV data does not yet exist in the corpus — the capstone must extrapolate from group-wide cohort curves rather than cite an Egypt number directly.

## Open Questions
- No source in the vault discloses an absolute LTV figure (in USD or local currency) for any customer segment or country — all LTV evidence is relative (multiples, percentage uplifts), never an absolute per-customer value.
- The goodwill-impairment cohort models (TLB-004, TLB-008) explicitly list the inputs used to value customer cohorts (retention/reorder rate, activity rate, average order size, commission rate) but do not disclose the resulting output values — the actual DCF cohort valuations are not in the corpus.
- No Egypt-specific cohort curve exists; the 3.5x–2.0x cohort multiples and the 136% gross-profit uplift are not disaggregated by country in any cited source.
- The vault does not state whether talabat pro's 136% gross-profit-per-customer uplift is net of the subscription fee discount/free-delivery cost funded partly by talabat versus Partners — this matters for computing a true incremental-LTV figure.

## Business Implications
- The absence of a disclosed absolute LTV figure means the capstone's financial modeling (value-driver tree, LTV:CAC framing) will need to construct an illustrative Egypt LTV estimate, which — per the project's standing instruction — must be clearly labeled as synthetic/estimated rather than presented as a disclosed talabat number.
- The 136% gross-profit-per-customer uplift from talabat pro (even though UAE-cohort-based) is the strongest single justification in the corpus for prioritizing subscription-driven retention investment over broader acquisition spend in the Egypt business case.
- The cohort-model inputs disclosed in TLB-004/TLB-008 (retention/reorder rate, activity rate, average order size, commission rate) give the capstone a defensible, source-grounded structure for building an illustrative Egypt LTV formula, even without the underlying output numbers.
