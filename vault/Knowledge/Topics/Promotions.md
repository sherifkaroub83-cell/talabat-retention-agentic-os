# Promotions

## Overview
talabat's promotional toolkit, as evidenced in the vault, is not primarily blanket discounting but a set of named, structured products: talabat Rewards (points-based, with an 18% adoption rate and >15% order-frequency uplift among first-time redeemers, TLB-026, page 134), talabat DineOut Deals (restaurant dine-out discovery/discounts), talabat PostPaid (deferred-payment FinTech, associated with a 14% order-frequency increase post-adoption per the Products entity list), and the co-branded ADCB credit card (cashback and free delivery, bundling a "tailored version" of talabat pro, TLB-001, page 13). Partner-funded savings — distinct from talabat-funded vouchering — reached an "all-time high of 7% of GMV" in Q1 2026 (TLB-020, page 7) and totaled more than AED 1,567.7 million over a 12-month period (TLB-023, page 6), indicating Partners co-fund a meaningful share of promotional value. Separately, talabat-funded vouchering is tracked as part of CARC (Customer Acquisition and Retention Costs): USD 89mn in 2025 vouchering spend, part of a total CARC of USD 155mn / 1.6% of GMV (TLB-002, page 21).

## Why It Matters
The corpus draws a consistent distinction between promotions that build durable engagement (Rewards, PostPaid, DineOut Deals — each tied to a specific order-frequency uplift figure) versus generic voucher spend (tracked as a cost line within CARC, not framed as a loyalty investment). This distinction matters directly for an AI-driven Egypt retention strategy: it supports recommending AI-targeted, product-embedded promotions (personalized rewards timing, PostPaid-style incentives) over broad, margin-eroding vouchering, consistent with talabat's own stated Q1 2026 shift toward "increased incentives to retain medium and high-value customers" (TLB-011, page 2) rather than blanket discounts.

## Open Questions
- The vault does not disclose Egypt-specific promotional data: no Egypt voucher spend, no Egypt DineOut Deals or PostPaid adoption figures, and no Egypt Rewards redemption rate.
- CARC is disclosed only at the group level (USD 155mn / 1.6% of GMV, 2025); the corpus does not break out how much of talabat-funded vouchering versus customer marketing cost is allocated to Egypt.
- The corpus does not state whether talabat PostPaid or the co-branded credit card (ADCB, UAE-specific) have Egypt equivalents — Subscription.md notes a co-branded card was separately launched in Egypt with Commercial International Bank (CIB), but no promotional/order-frequency data is disclosed for it.
- It is not clear from the vault whether the >15% order-frequency uplift for first-time Rewards redeemers is causal (the promotion drove the behavior) or reflects self-selection (already-engaged customers redeem first).

## Business Implications
- talabat's own cost taxonomy (CARC separating "vouchering" from "customer marketing") gives the capstone a template for proposing that Egypt-specific promotional spend be tracked and evaluated the same way — as an investment with a measurable frequency/retention return, not just a discount cost.
- The Rewards and PostPaid frequency-uplift figures, while not Egypt-specific, support recommending that any AI-driven Egypt promotions strategy prioritize personalized, product-embedded incentives (timed rewards, deferred payment) over broad discount vouchers.
- Because Partner-funded savings (as opposed to talabat-funded vouchers) reached an all-time-high share of GMV group-wide in Q1 2026, the capstone could reasonably recommend exploring Partner co-funded promotional mechanisms for Egypt to preserve margin while still funding retention-oriented offers — though no Egypt-specific Partner-funding data exists to validate feasibility there.
