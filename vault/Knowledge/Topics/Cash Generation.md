# Cash Generation

## Overview
talabat tracks cash generation primarily through Adjusted Free Cash Flow (FCF) and a Cash Conversion Ratio (Adjusted FCF as a % of Adjusted EBITDA), both at Group level. FY2024 Adjusted FCF was USD 462.4mn (6.23% of GMV), a 93.07% cash conversion ratio (TLB-029); FY2025 Adjusted FCF rose to USD 559mn (+21% y/y, 5.9% of GMV), with cash conversion easing slightly to 91% (TLB-002, pages 17, 22). Net cash stood at USD 591mn at end-2025, +83% versus end-2024, with zero external financial debt (TLB-002, page 23). The audited statement of cash flows for the since-inception period through 31 December 2025 (~16 months) shows net cash generated from operating activities of USD 879.7mn, net cash generated from investing activities of USD 259.9mn, and net cash used in financing activities of USD (368.9)mn (TLB-008, page 12). Q1 2026 Adjusted FCF was USD 104mn (3.9% of GMV, +7% y/y), with net cash exceeding USD 693mn by end of Q1 2026 (TLB-020, pages 11, 16); FY2026 guidance is USD 370–400mn (3.2–3.6% of GMV) (TLB-020, page 14).

## Why It Matters
talabat's cash generation is strong enough to simultaneously fund the USD 175 million 2026 investment programme, a 90%-of-net-income dividend policy (USD 421mn paid for FY2025, TLB-002, page 8), and an active share buyback of up to 5% of issued shares running May 2026–April 2028 (TLB-021) — meaning the capital-allocation question this capstone answers is not "can talabat afford to invest," but "how should an already-affordable pool of capital be allocated across Everyday App and Food-leadership initiatives, and across markets." That the company holds zero external financial debt and a growing net cash position also means the USD 175 million programme is self-funded from operating cash generation, not external financing — directly relevant to how the business plan should frame funding risk.

## Open Questions
- No document discloses Adjusted Free Cash Flow, cash conversion, or net cash at the GCC, non-GCC, or Egypt segment level — cash generation is a Group-level metric only throughout the corpus.
- The audited operating/investing/financing cash-flow breakdown (TLB-008, page 12) is a since-inception (~16-month) cumulative figure, not a clean calendar-year FY2025 figure, and is not reconciled anywhere in the corpus to the calendar-year Adjusted FCF figure (USD 559mn) reported in the Directors' Report narrative — the two are not directly comparable without further disclosure.
- A sell-side consensus source (TLB-028, secondary) shows FY2025A Free Cash Flow of USD 667mn, a different figure from the USD 559mn Adjusted FCF talabat itself discloses (TLB-002) — the corpus does not explain this discrepancy, and as unconsolidated `_raw` working-file content the consensus figure is not independently citable from the semantic layer; it is flagged here only as an unreconciled data-quality caveat, not asserted as fact.
- The corpus does not disclose how talabat prioritizes cash generation across its three visible uses (growth investment, dividends, buybacks) — no stated capital-allocation waterfall or hierarchy among them appears in any source (see [[Capital Allocation and Investment Governance]]).

## Business Implications
- Because the USD 175 million programme is small relative to disclosed annual cash generation (Adjusted FCF of USD 559mn in FY2025 alone, before dividends and buybacks), the business plan does not need to argue for the affordability of the programme — the constraint the plan should focus on is allocation quality (which markets, which initiative categories), not capital availability.
- The absence of segment-level cash-generation data means any Egypt-specific "self-funding capacity" claim in the business plan would need to be inferred from Egypt's disclosed net-profit figures (see [[Segment Reporting]]) rather than a directly disclosed Egypt cash-flow figure, and must be labeled as an estimate.
- That talabat is simultaneously investing, paying a 90%-of-net-income dividend, and buying back shares is evidence management does not treat growth investment and shareholder returns as mutually exclusive — a useful precedent for the business plan to cite if recommending incremental (not reallocated-from-elsewhere) investment.
