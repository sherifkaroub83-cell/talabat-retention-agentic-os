# FinTech

## Overview
FinTech is one of talabat's four named business units (alongside Food, Groceries & Retail, and AdTech), covering talabat PostPaid (buy-now-pay-later/deferred payment), talabat Pay (a closed-loop wallet), digital wallets used for rapid refunds and corporate allowances, and co-branded credit cards issued with local banking partners (TLB-001, page 125; TLB-002, page 13; TLB-015, page 110; TLB-026, page 114). Through the 2024 Capital Markets Day, PostPaid was disclosed as "live" only in the five GCC markets (UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman), with non-GCC expansion explicitly flagged as "subject to regulatory approvals" (TLB-015, pages 25, 74, 103) — Egypt was not included. The FY2025 Annual Report records that expansion happening: talabat PostPaid launched in Egypt and a co-branded credit card was issued with Commercial International Bank (CIB), Egypt's largest private-sector bank, described as talabat's "first FinTech expansion outside the UAE" (TLB-002, pages 14, 19). FinTech GMV penetration (PostPaid plus the ADCB co-branded card) rose from 5.6% (June 2024) to 6.1% (June 2025) (TLB-018, page 5).

## Why It Matters
FinTech is the corpus's clearest example of a business-unit-level product with a directly measured retention effect — PostPaid is associated with a 14% increase in order frequency post-adoption (TLB-001, page 18) and is explicitly described as "accretive to order frequency and customer retention" (TLB-001, page 9) — and it is also the one initiative category in the corpus where a concrete, dated non-GCC market entry (Egypt PostPaid and the CIB card) has actually occurred rather than merely being guided toward. For the capital-allocation decision, this makes FinTech a candidate Everyday App sub-initiative with disclosed precedent for non-GCC (specifically Egypt) rollout, distinct from talabat mart or talabat pro, both of which are discussed at Group level without a similarly concrete Egypt-specific FinTech launch narrative elsewhere.

## Open Questions
- No source discloses FinTech's revenue or profitability contribution as a standalone business unit — Subscription fee & Other Income bundles FinTech with talabat pro subscription revenue, so FinTech's own economics cannot be isolated from the disclosed revenue lines.
- The corpus does not state whether talabat Pay (the closed-loop wallet) has launched in Egypt or any non-GCC market — only PostPaid and the CIB card are confirmed for Egypt.
- No document explains what regulatory approvals were needed for the Egypt PostPaid/CIB launch, how long the process took, or whether Jordan and Iraq (the other two non-GCC markets) are next — the corpus shows the Egypt outcome, not the expansion decision process behind it.
- The FinTech GMV-penetration figure (5.6%→6.1%) is Group-wide; no country-level or GCC-vs-non-GCC breakdown of FinTech penetration is disclosed anywhere in the corpus.

## Business Implications
- Because Egypt already has a disclosed, dated FinTech launch (PostPaid + CIB card) with a directly measured frequency-uplift mechanism from the same product category, a capital-allocation recommendation to expand FinTech further in Egypt (or into Jordan/Iraq) would be extending a proven mechanism into adjacent non-GCC markets, not proposing an unproven new lever — a stronger evidentiary position than several other Everyday App sub-initiatives.
- The absence of standalone FinTech revenue/profitability disclosure means any Egypt-specific FinTech ROI case in the business plan must be built on the order-frequency proxy (14% uplift, Group-wide basis) rather than a disclosed dollar return, and must be labeled as an estimate accordingly.
- FinTech's GCC-first sequencing (proven for years before any non-GCC launch) is a template the business plan can point to for how talabat itself has historically staged multi-market product rollouts — relevant to how a Food-leadership or Everyday App initiative might be sequenced across the remaining seven non-Egypt markets.
