# Revenue Drivers

## Overview
Revenue Drivers is the causal-mechanics view of what actually moves talabat's top line, as distinct from the raw revenue figures in [[Revenue]] (Facts). The corpus consistently explains revenue growth as GMV growth combined with take-rate expansion: Management Revenue rose from 39.8% of GMV (FY2024) toward 40–41% (FY2025) and further in Q1 2026, driven by a shifting mix across four fee lines — commission fees, delivery & service fees, subscription fee & other income, and advertising & listing fees (TLB-001, page 27; TLB-002, page 20; TLB-011, page 4). In multiple periods revenue growth explicitly outpaced GMV growth, and the corpus names the specific causes each time: AdTech upside and tMart's rising revenue share in 2024 (TLB-012, page 19); higher subscription fees and tMart contribution in Q1 2025 (TLB-013, page 18); and higher talabat mart share plus adtech margins in Q1 2026, partly offset by lower commission rates and rising talabat pro adoption (TLB-014, page 15).

## Why It Matters
This is the topic that most directly operationalizes the capstone's "AI-driven retention" thesis in financial terms: the corpus shows subscription (talabat pro) and rewards each carry a documented order-frequency uplift (see [[Orders]], [[Relationship_Map]] section 6), meaning retention mechanics are not just a customer-experience lever but a named revenue driver — subscription is "monetized twice," once as a direct fee and once via the extra orders it generates. Advertising is also flagged as structurally under-monetized (3.3–3.5% of GMV against a ~7% global benchmark, TLB-001 page 21; TLB-002 page 10), which matters for an Egypt strategy because a fast-growing, increasingly loyal customer base is exactly the precondition for advertiser demand to scale toward that benchmark.

## Open Questions
- The corpus does not provide an Egypt-specific revenue-driver decomposition (how much of Egypt's ~81% YoY segment revenue growth in Q1 2026 is GMV growth vs. take-rate improvement vs. talabat pro's February 2025 Egypt launch) — this would need to be estimated, not cited, if used in the business plan.
- TLB-014 states 2026 revenue will shift to IFRS-only reporting, discontinuing the "Management Revenue" construct used throughout FY2021–2025 — the vault does not indicate how this affects comparability of the revenue-driver narrative going forward.
- The corpus quantifies AdTech's EBITDA impact (~USD 14mn incremental since Jan 2023, TLB-026 page 133) but does not isolate an equivalent Egypt-specific advertising revenue or EBITDA figure.

## Business Implications
- Because subscription (talabat pro) is documented as a dual revenue driver (fee + frequency), an Egypt retention strategy centered on talabat pro adoption has a direct, evidence-backed revenue case, not just a retention-KPI case.
- AdTech's under-penetration relative to the ~7% GMV benchmark suggests that as Egypt's active-partner base grows, retention-driven engagement (more frequent, more loyal customers) increases the inventory advertisers will pay for — a second-order revenue lever tied to retention.
- The repeated finding that grocery/G&R mix growth dilutes blended take rate and EBITDA margin means any retention strategy that pushes Egypt customers toward multi-vertical (G&R) engagement should be evaluated against this margin trade-off, not assumed to be purely additive to profitability.
