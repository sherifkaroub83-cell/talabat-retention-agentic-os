# Logistics

## Overview
Logistics is talabat's delivery-execution layer, spanning technology (dispatch algorithms, real-time GPS tracking, dynamic route optimisation, a rider staffing algorithm — all licensed from Delivery Hero), the rider fleet, and the third-party logistics (3PL) providers that source most of that fleet ([[Logistics|Facts/Logistics]], TLB-002, page 12, 16). talabat operates as principal in delivery — "primarily responsible for carrying out the delivery and controls the delivery service before it is transferred to the orderer" (TLB-004, page 38) — distinct from the agent model it uses for restaurant/marketplace commissions. Average delivery time improved from ~37 minutes (2020) to under 30 minutes across key markets by 2024-2025, with system reliability around 99.95% and on-time delivery improving from ~67% to ~78% over the same period (TLB-015, page 42, 82).

## Why It Matters
Logistics performance (speed, reliability) is a direct input to customer experience, and the corpus explicitly credits "AI-driven logistics improvements" with enhancing delivery times and cost-efficiency (TLB-001, page 10) and cites improved logistics efficiency as a driver of Adjusted EBITDA margin expansion (TLB-012, page 21; TLB-016, page 13) — meaning logistics is simultaneously a retention lever (service quality) and a margin lever, both directly relevant to the capstone's AI-driven retention business case for Egypt.

## Open Questions
- No Egypt-specific average delivery time, on-time rate, or Rider Safety Score is disclosed; the closest Egypt-specific logistics fact is the 200-EV pilot programme (end-2025) and Egypt's inclusion among the countries where the top-seven 3PL providers represent ~50% of the rider fleet (TLB-026, page 51).
- The corpus does not quantify the cost or service-level trade-off between 3PL-sourced and any directly-managed rider capacity.
- No fact directly links logistics KPIs (delivery time, reliability) to churn or retention rates — the connection is asserted qualitatively by management (TLB-001, page 10) rather than measured.

## Business Implications
- Because Cairo is named as one of talabat's two core tech hubs building the logistics/service technology stack (TLB-023, page 7), Egypt already has in-house technical capacity relevant to any AI-driven logistics-linked retention initiative — a structural asset the business plan can build on.
- The documented delivery-time and on-time-rate improvement trend (platform-wide) supports treating logistics reliability as a credible, evidenced lever for the retention strategy, though Egypt-specific baseline performance data is not available and should be flagged as a gap rather than assumed equivalent to the platform average.
- The margin linkage (logistics efficiency → EBITDA expansion) means any proposed logistics-linked retention investment (e.g., AI dispatch improvements) can be framed with a dual retention-and-margin business case, consistent with the McKinsey value-driver-tree approach the business plan requires.
