# Profitability

## Overview
Profitability in the corpus is tracked through Adjusted EBITDA, Net Income, Adjusted Net Income, and Gross Profit, each conventionally expressed as a margin of GMV. Group Adjusted EBITDA rose from ~USD 500mn (6.7% of GMV, +55% YoY, FY2024) to USD 615mn (6.5% of GMV, +24% YoY, FY2025), while Net Income rose from USD 346mn (4.7% of GMV, +64% YoY, FY2024) to USD 464mn (4.9% of GMV, +34% YoY, FY2025) (TLB-004, page 3; TLB-008, page 3; Facts/Marketplace.md). Gross profit was USD 915mn (12.3% of GMV) in FY2024 and USD 1,124–1,418mn in FY2025 depending on pro-forma vs. audited basis (Facts/Marketplace.md, TLB-002, page 20). Egypt's profitability is the standout data point: its newly disclosed segment swung from a near-breakeven USD 0.5mn profit before tax (Q1 2025, restated) to USD 9.4mn (Q1 2026) — an inflection, not just growth — and posted a full-year FY2025 net profit of USD 44.4mn on USD 509.9mn revenue (TLB-010, page 20; TLB-002, page 111).

## Why It Matters
Egypt's swing from near-breakeven to solidly profitable in roughly a year — coinciding with talabat pro's February 2025 Egypt launch and FinTech expansion (co-branded card with CIB) — is the clearest evidence in the corpus that Egypt has crossed an inflection point where retention investment and profitability can now compound together, rather than retention spend competing against a loss-making base. Group-level Adjusted EBITDA growth (55% in FY2024) also outpaced revenue growth, and the corpus attributes part of this operating leverage to AI-driven personalization (an estimated USD 14mn+ to USD 30mn+ incremental EBITDA impact, per [[Relationship_Map]] section 4) — directly linking the capstone's AI/retention thesis to a quantified profitability outcome.

## Open Questions
- The corpus's forward-looking Free Cash Flow and dividend consensus estimates (from TLB-027/TLB-028) exist only in the unconsolidated `_raw` working files, not in any Facts/*.md file — they are not citable from the semantic layer as it currently stands, so this note cannot state analyst forward profitability expectations beyond the FY2025 actual dividend (USD 421mn, 90% payout) and the audited FY2025 figures.
- No document decomposes Egypt's profitability swing into its component causes (talabat pro launch, FinTech expansion, GMV growth, take-rate change, cost efficiency) — the corpus shows the outcome (breakeven → USD 9.4mn PBT) but not an attributed cause-and-effect chain.
- The vault does not state an Egypt-specific EBITDA margin or Adjusted EBITDA figure (only revenue, gross profit, profit before tax, and net profit are disclosed at the Egypt-segment level) — EBITDA-level margin comparisons between Egypt and the Group are not directly citable.

## Business Implications
- Egypt's documented swing to profitability removes a common objection to retention investment (i.e., "this market doesn't have the margin to invest in retention yet") — the segment data shows Egypt is now profit-generative, giving the business plan a credible basis to propose incremental retention/AI spend there.
- Because AI-driven personalization has a directly quantified group-level EBITDA impact, the business plan can position an Egypt-specific AI retention initiative as an extension of an already-proven profitability mechanism rather than a speculative one.
- The corpus's flag that grocery/G&R mix growth dilutes Adjusted EBITDA margin means any Egypt retention strategy that leans on cross-selling into groceries should model the margin trade-off explicitly, not assume retention gains are margin-neutral.
