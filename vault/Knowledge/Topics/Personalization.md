# Personalization

## Overview
Personalisation at talabat is the customer-facing output of the AI/ML layer: once a customer has placed roughly six orders, talabat's machine-learning models have enough signal to build a personalised profile and begin tailoring what that customer sees — cross-selling verticals, surfacing item-level recommendations, and choosing the right moment to present a talabat pro or talabat Rewards offer (TLB-001, page 22-23; TLB-002, page 15; TLB-023, page 8). This is distinct from [[AI]] as an umbrella capability (which also covers logistics, fraud, and ad-targeting) and from [[Recommendation Systems]] (the specific ranking mechanism) — personalisation is the customer-experience and business-impact layer that sits on top of both.

## Why It Matters
Personalisation is where AI investment converts most directly into retention economics: the estimated EBITDA contribution of the personalisation/ranking algorithm rose from USD 14mn+ p.a. to USD 30mn+ p.a. between FY2024 and FY2025 (TLB-001, page 23; TLB-002, page 15), and the same mechanism is explicitly credited with timing talabat pro and talabat Rewards offers "at the right time for customers to benefit from" (TLB-023, page 8) — i.e., personalisation isn't just a UX feature, it is the delivery mechanism for the corpus's two named loyalty products. For Egypt, where the customer base is newer and less mature in its ordering history than the GCC, the 6-order threshold implies a specific early-tenure window where a customer has not yet "unlocked" personalisation — a natural focal point for a retention intervention.

## Open Questions
- The corpus states personalisation activates after ~6 orders but does not disclose what fraction of Egypt's active customer base has crossed that threshold, nor how long it typically takes an Egyptian customer to reach 6 orders versus a GCC customer.
- There is no disclosed churn or retention comparison between pre-personalisation (fewer than 6 orders) and post-personalisation customers — the corpus states the threshold and the aggregate EBITDA impact, but not a segmented before/after retention metric.
- No figure attributes any share of the USD 14mn→30mn+ EBITDA estimate to Egypt specifically.

## Business Implications
- The 6-order threshold is a concrete, cited "graduation point" the retention strategy can build an early-tenure engagement plan around — e.g., accelerating a new Egyptian customer's path to their 6th order through targeted incentives, since that is the corpus's own stated trigger for personalisation to activate.
- The documented shift from cuisine-level to item-level recommendations (TLB-002, page 15) indicates continued investment in personalisation granularity — a trend the strategy can argue should be extended and measured specifically in Egypt.
- Because personalisation is explicitly tied to loyalty-offer timing (pro, rewards), the strategy can position personalisation quality as a precondition for talabat pro conversion, not a parallel, independent initiative.
- The absence of Egypt-segmented personalisation-to-retention data is a clear primary-research opportunity the capstone can flag, per the project's standing instruction that synthetic/primary data may be used, clearly labeled, where corpus data is unavailable.
