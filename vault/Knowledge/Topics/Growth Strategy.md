# Growth Strategy

## Overview
talabat's growth strategy, as it appears across the corpus's most recent documents, centers on building the "Everyday App" — a 2026 investment programme of roughly USD 100-120mn (variously described as ">USD 100mn ecosystem investment," TLB-019, page 11, and "~USD 120mn," split into ~USD 75mn opex and ~USD 45mn capex, TLB-020, page 12/16) directed at scaling talabat mart dark stores, deepening the talabat pro loyalty programme, and expanding new verticals. The strategy leans heavily on the non-GCC segment (Jordan, Iraq, and — until FY2025 — Egypt), which has consistently grown GMV far faster than the mature GCC segment: +42% YoY in 2024 and +57% YoY in 2025, versus +20-22% for GCC over the same periods (see [[GMV]]). talabat itself frames Egypt and Iraq as "emerging countries with high growth rates," noting that as of the 2024 Capital Markets Day, talabat's footprint covered only ~42% of larger Egyptian cities (population >100k) and ~19% of larger Iraqi cities — an explicitly named geographic-expansion opportunity (TLB-015, page 101).

## Why It Matters
Retention is not treated as separate from growth in talabat's own strategy language — it is one of the named levers funded by the same 2026 investment programme, alongside acquisition and category penetration. Management's explicit 2026 objective to "retain high and medium value customers against partial or complete churn to competition" (TLB-002, page 14) sits directly inside the growth strategy, and the Everyday App thesis explicitly connects "frequency, basket size, and retention" to the company's ability "to scale its Everyday App across our markets" (TLB-020, page 16). For this capstone, that is the strongest available evidence that an Egypt retention strategy is not a niche initiative but a core component of how talabat itself defines growth for its newest standalone, fastest-growing major segment.

## Open Questions
- The corpus does not disclose how the USD 100-120mn 2026 Everyday App investment is allocated across individual countries — there is no Egypt-specific dollar figure to anchor a business-plan investment ask against.
- No fact quantifies the expected ROI, payback period, or specific retention-uplift target tied to the 2026 investment programme beyond directional language ("improving frequency, basket size, and retention").
- The Egypt/Iraq city-coverage figures (~42%/~19%) are from the 2024 Capital Markets Day deck; no more recent figure updates this for Egypt specifically, so current (2026) geographic penetration headroom in Egypt is not directly evidenced.
- FY2026 GMV growth guidance (11-14% cFX) is markedly lower than FY2025 actual (28%) — the corpus attributes this to "a larger base, more competitive environment, macro headwinds, and instashop dilution" (TLB-019, page 18) but does not separate out how much of the deceleration is Egypt-specific versus Group-wide.

## Business Implications
- Because talabat's own 2026 growth strategy explicitly funds retention (not just acquisition) as a lever, an AI-driven retention recommendation for Egypt is directly aligned with, rather than orthogonal to, management's stated capital-allocation priorities — this is a strong "build on existing strategy" framing for the business plan.
- Egypt's still-partial city coverage (as of the last cited data point) suggests the retention strategy should be explicitly sequenced alongside continued geographic expansion, rather than assuming a fully penetrated addressable market.
- The absence of country-level investment-allocation and ROI data means the business plan's financial case for Egypt-specific retention investment will need to build its own value-driver-tree estimate rather than cite a disclosed Egypt figure — and any such estimate should be clearly labeled as the capstone's own construction, consistent with the project's synthetic-data labeling standard.
- The deceleration in guided FY2026 GMV growth versus FY2025 actual reinforces the case that future growth will need to come increasingly from deepening engagement with the existing base (retention, multi-verticality, frequency) rather than pure new-market/new-customer acquisition — the exact thesis this capstone is built to support.
