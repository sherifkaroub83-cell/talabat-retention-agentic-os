# Capital Allocation and Investment Governance

## Overview
**This note is thin by design, not by oversight — the corpus discloses talabat's capital-allocation *outputs* (a headline number, a two-way split, a margin bridge) but essentially none of its internal *governance mechanics* (who evaluates, approves, or stage-gates a specific investment).** What is disclosed: the Board approved a USD 175 million 2026 investment programme, split into ~USD 120 million for "Everyday App" initiatives (~USD 75mn opex + ~USD 45mn capex, covering tMart scaling, talabat pro strengthening, and new-vertical expansion) and ~USD 55 million for "Food-leadership" initiatives (TLB-020, pages 12, 16). This figure was disclosed progressively across three documents: TLB-019 (13 Feb 2026) first names the full "$175mn Board-approved programme" alongside ">$100mn of investment in 2026" (TLB-019, page 11); TLB-002, the FY2025 Annual Report (31 Mar 2026), separately describes ">USD 100mn incremental investment... split between scaling talabat mart and strengthening talabat pro" without naming the USD 175mn total or the Food-leadership component (TLB-002, page 10); TLB-020 (12 May 2026) gives the full USD 120mn/USD 55mn breakdown. Management frames the spend explicitly as investment, not cost: "These are investments rather than costs because the growth they generate is expected to offset the margin impact over time" (TLB-020, page 16), with the stated exit criterion of a "more loyal customer base" (TLB-002, page 10).

## Why It Matters
For a capstone whose entire deliverable is a recommendation on how to allocate this same USD 175 million programme, the honest finding is that talabat's own disclosed rationale is directional and outcome-framed (frequency, basket size, retention; "these are investments not costs") rather than a described evaluation process with named criteria, thresholds, or approval stages — meaning the business plan cannot claim to be replicating or reverse-engineering talabat's internal capital-allocation methodology, only proposing a defensible, evidence-grounded allocation logic of its own. This is directly consistent with [[Decision-Making Process]]'s broader finding that the corpus shows decision *outputs*, not decision *mechanics*, and this note is the capital-allocation-specific instance of that same gap.

## Open Questions
- **No document in the corpus describes an internal capital-allocation committee, an investment-approval threshold, a stage-gate process, or how a specific initiative (country-level or category-level) moves from proposal to funded line item.** This is the single largest, load-bearing gap for this note and for the capstone's own decision problem: the plan must propose an allocation logic, not describe or validate talabat's actual one.
- No document discloses how the USD 175 million total, or its ~USD 120mn/~USD 55mn split, was itself arrived at — no unit-economics model, ROI hurdle rate, or payback-period criterion is named anywhere in the corpus for this programme.
- No document discloses any country-level or GCC/non-GCC/Egypt-level breakdown of either the Everyday App or the Food-leadership component — the capstone's specific decision (how to allocate within these categories across markets) has literally no disclosed precedent to check itself against.
- The corpus does not reconcile why TLB-002 (Annual Report 2025, 31 March 2026) describes a narrower ">USD 100mn" investment without naming the USD 175mn total or the Food-leadership component that TLB-019 (13 February 2026, six weeks earlier) had already disclosed — whether this is an intentional annual-report framing choice, an editorial oversight, or reflects a scope difference is not stated.
- No document explains whether or how the Board revisits or could reallocate the USD 175 million programme intra-year if early results diverge from plan (the Q1 2026 results already show a larger-than-expected EBITDA margin impact) — only the initial approval and the resulting margin bridge are visible.

## Business Implications
- Because no internal approval process, threshold, or country-level precedent is disclosed, the business plan's investment-governance section must be explicit that its recommended stage-gate/KPI framework is the capstone's own proposed mechanism, built to satisfy the visible Group-level Board-approval pattern — not a description or validation of an actual talabat process, consistent with the Problem Charter's instruction that recommendations be staged proposals gated for human approval, not false-precision claims.
- Management's own "investment, not cost" framing and stated exit criterion ("a more loyal customer base") give the business plan legitimate, source-grounded language to justify a margin-dilutive allocation recommendation, provided the plan is explicit that this is talabat's stated rationale for the existing USD 175mn programme, not evidence the same rationale applies automatically to a different intra-programme allocation.
- The total absence of country-level allocation precedent means any Egypt-specific (or GCC/non-GCC-specific) share of the USD 175 million that the business plan proposes must be presented strictly as a range or staged-funding proposal, not a point estimate — directly matching the Problem Charter's own evidence-limitations framing.
