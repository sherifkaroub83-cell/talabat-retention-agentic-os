# GCC vs non-GCC

## Overview
This note is the canonical definitional reference for talabat's GCC / non-GCC split: **GCC** comprises United Arab Emirates, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain and Oman; **non-GCC** comprises Jordan and Iraq, with Egypt included in non-GCC through FY2024 and most 2025 interim filings but carved out into its own standalone reportable segment starting with the FY2025 audited financials (TLB-008) — see [[Segment Reporting]] for the accounting/disclosure-history mechanics of that change; this note is for what the split *means* and what is disclosed at each level, not for how the disclosure changed over time. GCC is structurally dominant: 82% of pro-forma FY2025 GMV (USD 6,332mn of a total that also includes the now-separated Egypt figure, TLB-002, page 18) and, per IFRS 8 disclosure, "more than 75%" of Group revenue and assets throughout the corpus (TLB-004, page 37). Non-GCC (Jordan + Iraq only, post-Egypt-carve-out) is smaller but grows faster: FY2025 GMV +57% y/y vs. GCC's +22% (TLB-019, page 14); Q1 2026 non-GCC GMV +52% y/y at 21% of Group GMV, up from 16% a year earlier (TLB-011, page 2). The clearest structural-maturity gap is per-capita order frequency: 2023 monthly orders per capita were 1.28x in GCC versus 0.13x in non-GCC — a ~10x difference — against a Group average of 0.42x (TLB-015, page 12; TLB-026, page 121).

## Why It Matters
Because talabat's own disclosure boundary is GCC / non-GCC / (now) Egypt — never "MENA" and rarely a full eight-country breakdown — any capital-allocation recommendation this capstone makes about markets outside Egypt (i.e., how much of the USD 175 million programme might logically flow to Jordan or Iraq versus GCC markets) can only be grounded at the non-GCC level, not at the individual Jordan or Iraq level, because the corpus discloses no country-specific figures for either. This note exists so every other Topic Note and the Business Plan itself can point to one place for "what does GCC vs. non-GCC actually mean, and what's disclosed at each level" rather than restating the segment definition inconsistently.

## Open Questions
- The corpus never discloses a Jordan-specific or Iraq-specific GMV, revenue, or profitability figure — every non-GCC number after Egypt's carve-out is a Jordan+Iraq combined figure, meaning this note (and the capstone) cannot distinguish between the two remaining non-GCC markets.
- No document discloses whether the GCC vs. non-GCC per-capita order-frequency gap (1.28x vs. 0.13x, 2023 basis) has narrowed since then — no more recent per-capita frequency split by segment appears in the corpus.
- The corpus does not disclose the USD 175 million 2026 investment programme's allocation across GCC vs. non-GCC (or Egypt), only its split between Everyday App and Food-leadership initiative categories (see [[Capital Allocation and Investment Governance]]).
- It is not stated whether non-GCC's disproportionately higher CIT rate structure (Egypt 22.5%, Jordan 21%, vs. 15% across all five GCC markets, TLB-002, page 22) is factored into how talabat frames non-GCC investment returns internally.

## Business Implications
- The ~10x per-capita order-frequency gap between GCC and non-GCC (2023 basis) is the strongest disclosed evidence that non-GCC markets — Egypt included — are earlier in their demand-maturity curve, supporting a business-plan argument for continued category-penetration and multi-vertical investment in non-GCC rather than treating it as a fully "grown" market segment like GCC.
- Because non-GCC's higher GMV growth rate (57% FY2025) coexists with a much lower absolute order-frequency base, the business plan should frame non-GCC investment cases around volume/frequency upside rather than margin optimization, which is better evidenced in the mature GCC segment.
- The complete absence of a disclosed country-level (or even GCC/non-GCC-level) breakdown of the USD 175 million investment programme means any Jordan/Iraq/GCC allocation the business plan proposes must be an explicitly labeled, reasoned range — never presented as if it mirrors an actual talabat allocation decision.
