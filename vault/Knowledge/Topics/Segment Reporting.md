# Segment Reporting

## Overview
talabat's IFRS 8 reportable-segment structure changed materially over the corpus's timeline. Through FY2024 and most 2025 interim filings, the Group reported two segments: **GCC** (UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman — consistently >75% of revenue and assets) and a residual **"Other segments" / non-GCC** bucket combining Jordan, Iraq, **and Egypt**, none of which individually met IFRS 8's 10% disclosure thresholds (TLB-004, page 37; Facts/Business_Units.md). Starting with the FY2025 audited financials (TLB-008) and the FY2025 Annual Report (TLB-002), **Egypt independently crossed the IFRS 8 quantitative thresholds and became its own standalone reportable segment**, distinct from both GCC and the now Egypt-excluded non-GCC (Jordan + Iraq) bucket. This is carried forward into the Q1 2026 financial statements (TLB-010), which report quarterly Egypt segment revenue, external revenue, and profit before tax alongside GCC and non-GCC.

## Why It Matters
This is the single most consequential disclosure-related finding for the capstone: before FY2025, Egypt-specific financial performance was structurally invisible in talabat's reporting — analysts and researchers (including this capstone) had only the one-off IPO-prospectus datapoint that Egypt was 6% of 2023 revenue but just 1% of 2023 Adjusted EBITDA (TLB-026, page 93; TLB-015, page 116). Segment-level disclosure now exists precisely because Egypt has grown large enough to require it — and it arrives at almost the exact same moment talabat pro launched in Egypt (February 2025) and Egypt swung from near-breakeven to solidly profitable, giving the capstone a genuinely new, citable evidence base that simply did not exist in earlier filings.

## Open Questions
- The corpus never explains, in its own words, exactly which IFRS 8 quantitative threshold Egypt crossed (revenue, profit, or assets) or by how much — it states the outcome (Egypt now meets the threshold) without the underlying calculation.
- No document in the corpus discloses an Egypt-specific GMV figure, even though Egypt now has its own segment P&L — this means the capstone cannot cite a precise Egypt GMV number and must rely on revenue-based proxies or explicitly flag the gap.
- The restated Q1 2025 Egypt comparator in TLB-010 (USD 80.3mn revenue vs. the original, unrestated period) is not explained — it is unclear from the vault whether the restatement reflects a segment-boundary change, an accounting policy change, or something else.
- It is not stated in the corpus whether Egypt's new segment status is expected to be permanent (i.e., whether it could in theory fall back below the IFRS 8 threshold) or what would trigger re-consolidation into "non-GCC."

## Business Implications
- The Egypt segment's newly visible profitability (swinging positive within roughly a year of talabat pro's Egypt launch) gives the business plan a legitimate, citable financial argument that Egypt is not a subsidized growth market but a standalone profit center capable of funding its own retention investment.
- Because Egypt-specific GMV is still undisclosed, any GMV-based sizing of the Egypt retention opportunity in the business plan will need to be estimated from revenue and the disclosed take-rate ranges — this should be explicitly labeled as an estimate, per the project's standing instruction on synthetic/derived data.
- The timing coincidence between Egypt's segment-reporting debut and talabat pro's Egypt launch is circumstantial, not proven causal, in the corpus — the business plan should frame this as a correlated inflection point to investigate further, not assert it as a demonstrated causal retention outcome.
