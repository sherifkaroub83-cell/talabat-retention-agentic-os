# Multi-Verticality

## Overview
Multi-verticality is talabat's term for customers who order across more than one of its verticals
(principally Food plus Groceries & Retail), and it is the corpus's most consistently quantified customer
-behaviour metric: multi-vertical customers order roughly 3-3.5x more frequently than food-only
customers (13.0 vs. 3.8 orders/month, July 2024; TLB-012, page 9; TLB-013, page 6), and their share of
total platform GMV has risen steadily — 68% (Dec 2024) → 73% (Dec 2025) → 76% (Mar 2026) (TLB-019, page
7; TLB-020, page 8). talabat's own stated customer journey is explicitly sequential: "Food → Grocery &
Retail → talabat pro sign-up" (TLB-013, page 6), positioning multi-vertical adoption as the mechanism
that converts a food-only customer into the more valuable, more retained customer segment.

## Why It Matters
Management names multi-verticality directly as the rationale for the entire "Everyday App" component of
the 2026 investment programme: "As customer behaviour becomes increasingly multi-vertical, improving
frequency, basket size, and retention position talabat to scale its Everyday App across our markets"
(TLB-014, page 19; TLB-020, page 16). This makes multi-verticality the connective evidence between the
2026 investment programme's dollar figure and its expected value-driver outcomes (order frequency, GMV,
retention) — any value driver tree the plan builds from Everyday App investment to financial outcomes
should treat multi-vertical adoption as the primary intermediate mechanism, not a secondary detail.

## Open Questions
- No source discloses multi-vertical adoption rates or GMV share by individual country — Egypt's specific multi-vertical penetration relative to the Group figure is not evidenced anywhere in the corpus.
- The corpus does not state whether the Food → G&R → talabat pro sequence holds in the same order, or at a similar conversion rate, across all 8 markets, or is a GCC-observed pattern generalized in company messaging.
- No fact quantifies the cost (marketing, incentive, or product-development spend) required to convert a food-only customer into a multi-vertical one, which would be needed to assess multi-vertical conversion as a discrete investable initiative rather than an observed correlation.
- The frequency comparison (13.0 vs. 3.8 orders/month) is dated to July 2024 and has not been refreshed with a more recent figure anywhere in the corpus, though the GMV-share trend (68%→73%→76%) is current through March 2026.

## Business Implications
- Multi-verticality is the strongest single evidenced link between the Everyday App investment and its intended financial outcomes, making it the natural anchor node for the plan's value driver tree connecting 2026 investment dollars to GMV/revenue/EBITDA.
- Because the frequency uplift and GMV-share evidence is Group-wide, any market-specific allocation recommendation that leans on multi-verticality as a rationale (e.g., "invest more in Egypt because multi-vertical customers are more valuable") must be explicit that the underlying behavioural evidence is not itself Egypt-specific.
- The rising multi-vertical GMV share trend (an accelerating, not merely stable, metric across three consecutive periods) supports treating multi-verticality as a genuine current growth lever rather than a mature, plateauing one — relevant to how the plan sequences pilot/scale-up recommendations.
- Given the correlation-not-causation limitation (no disclosed conversion-cost data), the plan should frame multi-vertical adoption as a KPI to monitor as capital is deployed, not as a guaranteed return already proven to result from the specific 2026 investment dollars.
