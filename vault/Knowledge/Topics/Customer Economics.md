# Customer Economics

## Overview
Customer Economics covers the recurring, per-order and per-month unit economics of a talabat customer — order frequency, monthly spend, the take-rate that converts GMV into revenue, and the cost lines (delivery cost, CARC) that sit between GMV and profit — as distinct from [[Customer Lifetime Value]], which covers the multi-year, cohort-compounding view of the same customer. At Group level, average order frequency was 6.7 orders/month in December 2025 (TLB-002, page 9), up from 6.5x (July 2024, TLB-015, page 13) and 6.0x (September 2023, TLB-026, page 26). Monthly spend differs sharply by engagement depth: food-only customers spent AED 194 (USD 53)/month at 3.8 orders, while multi-vertical (Food + Groceries & Retail) customers spent AED 814 (USD 222)/month at 12.8 orders — a >4x spend gap tied to a ~3.4x frequency gap (September 2024, TLB-026, pages 91, 122). Group revenue converts from GMV at roughly 38–41% (take rate), with commission fees (~12.8–16.2% of GMV), delivery/service fees (~8.3–14.8% of GMV), advertising (~3.3–9.4% of GMV) and subscription/other income as the component lines (TLB-011, page 4; TLB-019, page 13). Net delivery cost runs 6.0–6.3% of GMV and gross profit 11.9–12.3% of GMV (TLB-002, pages 17, 20).

## Why It Matters
For a capital-allocation decision between Everyday App and Food-leadership initiatives, unit economics is the mechanism that turns a customer-behaviour metric (frequency, multi-verticality) into a P&L outcome (take rate, gross margin) that the USD 175 million programme is meant to move. The frequency/spend gap between food-only and multi-vertical customers is the clearest disclosed evidence that cross-sell (an Everyday App lever) has a quantifiable per-customer economic payoff, while Customer Acquisition and Retention Costs (CARC) rising from USD 89mn (1.5% of GMV, 2023) to USD 155mn (1.6% of GMV, 2025) shows the cost side of funding that behaviour is also growing (TLB-001, page 28; TLB-002, page 21; see [[Retention]]).

## Open Questions
- No disclosed source breaks food-only vs. multi-vertical monthly spend down by country — the AED 194/AED 814 comparison (TLB-026) is Group-wide (September 2024 basis) and is not known to hold at the same magnitude in Egypt specifically.
- CARC and CAC are disclosed only at Group level (TLB-001, TLB-002) — there is no Egypt-specific or GCC-vs-non-GCC-specific acquisition/retention cost figure anywhere in the corpus.
- No source discloses a per-order or per-customer contribution-margin figure (revenue minus variable delivery/payment-processing cost per order) — only aggregate gross-profit-margin-of-GMV is available, which cannot be safely decomposed into a per-order figure without inventing an assumption.
- The corpus does not state whether the take-rate/cost-line ratios above hold at the Egypt-segment level; Egypt's segment P&L (see [[Segment Reporting]]) discloses revenue and gross profit but not GMV, so an Egypt-specific take rate cannot be calculated from disclosed figures alone.

## Business Implications
- The disclosed frequency/spend gap between food-only and multi-vertical customers is the strongest available per-customer economic argument for Everyday App-style cross-sell investment (talabat mart, talabat pro) over pure Food-leadership spend, if the goal is maximizing revenue per customer.
- Rising CARC as a share of GMV (1.5% → 1.6% of GMV, 2023–2025) means any allocation recommendation should treat retention/acquisition spend efficiency, not just its existence, as a evaluation criterion — per [[Promotions]] and `vault/Knowledge/Strategic/Cost Structure.md`'s framing.
- Because none of these unit-economics ratios are disclosed at the Egypt or non-GCC level, any Egypt-specific unit-economics figure used in the business plan's financial model must be built as an explicitly labeled estimate (per the project's standing synthetic-data instruction), not presented as a disclosed talabat number.
