# EBITDA

## Overview
talabat reports Group Adjusted EBITDA as its primary profitability metric, conventionally expressed as a margin of GMV: USD ~500mn (6.7% of GMV, +55% YoY) in FY2024, rising to USD 615mn (6.5% of GMV, +24% YoY) in FY2025 (TLB-004, page 3; TLB-008, page 3). The build-up runs GMV → Revenue (take rate ~38–41% of GMV, see [[Customer Economics]]) → Gross Profit (11.9–12.3% of GMV) → Adjusted EBITDA after operating costs, with talabat disclosing the fee-mix and cost-line components that bridge revenue to EBITDA rather than a single formula (TLB-002, pages 17, 20–21; see [[Financial Performance]] and [[Profitability]] for the fuller trend and margin narrative, not duplicated here). Q1 2026 Adjusted EBITDA fell to USD 130mn (4.8% of GMV, -9% y/y), a deliberate step-down management attributes explicitly to the USD 175 million 2026 investment programme rather than to underlying deterioration (TLB-020, pages 4, 11–12).

## Why It Matters
The corpus's most granular EBITDA disclosure is a margin bridge that names, in percentage-point terms, exactly how much of the FY2026 EBITDA margin compression is attributable to Everyday App investment versus Food-leadership investment — the only place in the corpus where the two components of the USD 175 million programme are directly tied to a quantified P&L effect. This makes EBITDA the metric through which the capstone's capital-allocation decision is most directly traceable to a disclosed financial outcome, even though the underlying initiative-level or country-level cost breakdown behind the bridge is not itself disclosed.

## Open Questions
- No document discloses an Egypt-specific, GCC-specific, or non-GCC-specific Adjusted EBITDA or EBITDA-margin figure — only revenue, gross profit, profit before tax and net profit are disclosed at the Egypt segment level (see [[Profitability]]'s Open Questions), so no segment-level EBITDA comparison is possible.
- The FY2026 margin bridge attributes -0.7pp (~USD 75mn) to Everyday App/tMart investment and -0.5pp (~USD 55mn) to Food-leadership investment (TLB-019, page 19), but no document explains how these percentage-point impacts were calculated or what specific cost lines (opex vs. capex, by initiative) sum to them.
- The Q1 2026 bridge (TLB-020, page 12) uses a different percentage-point split (-0.4% opex + -0.5% capex-related for Everyday App vs. -0.2% for Food-leadership) than the FY2026 full-year bridge in TLB-019 — the corpus does not reconcile why the quarterly and full-year bridges attribute the investment impact differently.
- No forward EBITDA consensus by country or segment exists in the citable Facts layer; only Group-level sell-side consensus (TLB-027, TLB-028) is available, and even that is secondary evidence.

## Business Implications
- Because the only disclosed link between the USD 175 million programme and EBITDA is a Group-level percentage-point bridge, the business plan's financial case for any specific allocation across markets or initiative categories will need to construct its own EBITDA-impact estimate rather than cite a disclosed country- or initiative-level figure — and must label that estimate accordingly.
- Management's own framing — that the FY2026 margin step-down is a deliberate investment choice, not deterioration — gives the business plan a legitimate basis to recommend continued or reallocated investment spend without needing to defend against a margin-decline narrative, provided the recommendation stays within the same "investment, not cost" logic talabat itself uses (see [[Capital Allocation and Investment Governance]]).
- The absence of segment-level EBITDA data is a genuine evidence gap the business plan should name explicitly when comparing the relative profitability return of GCC-focused versus non-GCC-focused (including Egypt) investment options, rather than assuming Group-level EBITDA dynamics apply uniformly across segments.
