# Quick Commerce

## Overview
Quick commerce at talabat is embodied by talabat mart (tMart) — talabat-owned, delivery-only dark stores targeting sub-30-minute grocery delivery — operated as a principal (owned-inventory, GMV-based) model rather than the commission-based marketplace agent model ([[Q-Commerce|Facts/Q-Commerce]], [[Delivery_Models]]). The store network grew from ~130 locations (end-2024) to ~160 across all 8 markets by 2025, with tMart GMV up 45-47% y/y and representing 31% of total revenue in 2025 (vs. 28% in 2024) at an effective ~95% take rate (TLB-002, pages 12, 14, 20). InstaShop, acquired for ~USD 32m, operates a related but largely non-overlapping hyper-local grocery model in UAE and Egypt (TLB-013, page 15).

## Why It Matters
Q-commerce is explicitly named in the 2026 guidance as one of two priority investment areas (alongside talabat pro), with talabat allocating >USD 100 million (later cited as ~USD 120m "Everyday App" investment) partly to "improving affordability to accelerate customer adoption" and "increasing store density to reinforce the speed-led value proposition" (TLB-002, page 10; TLB-009, page 2; TLB-011, page 2). For talabat Egypt's retention strategy, Q-commerce matters because it is a second, complementary use-case (grocery, not just food) that can extend engagement frequency with existing customers — and because grocery TAC penetration is still described as only "low single-digit" against a USD 150bn+ TAM, implying large headroom (TLB-014, page 19; TLB-020, page 16).

## Open Questions
- No Egypt-specific tMart store count, GMV, or penetration figure appears in the corpus — the 127-160 store footprint is reported only at the 8-market aggregate level (TLB-026, page 130-131; TLB-002, page 14).
- The vault does not disclose whether talabat pro benefits or discounts extend to tMart/Q-Commerce orders specifically, which would be directly relevant to a cross-vertical retention design.
- InstaShop's Egypt-specific GMV or partner count is not broken out from its combined UAE+Egypt figures.

## Business Implications
- Q-Commerce represents a genuine second engagement surface for retention design in Egypt — a customer retained via Food ordering could plausibly be cross-sold into grocery, though the corpus does not evidence this cross-sell rate directly.
- The stated 2026 priority on "affordability" as a lever for quick-commerce adoption suggests price-sensitivity is a known constraint in this vertical, relevant to any Egypt-specific retention/pricing recommendation.
- The lower take-rate/margin profile of G&R versus Food (explicitly flagged as an EBITDA drag) means a retention strategy that shifts order mix toward Q-Commerce should account for a margin trade-off, not treat all order growth as equally profitable.
