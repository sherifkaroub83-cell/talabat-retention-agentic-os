# Food Leadership

## Overview
"Food leadership" is talabat's name for the smaller (~USD 55 million) of the two components of its 2026
investment programme, explicitly targeted at defending talabat's #1 category position in restaurant
food delivery against "both new entrants and incumbents" (TLB-020, page 16). Rather than matching
competitor discounts or vouchers, management states the investment goes toward customer value
proposition (CVP): "on the customer side, we focus on retaining medium- and high-value customers through
our subscription programme and targeted incentives" (TLB-014, page 19; TLB-020, page 16). FY2026
guidance separately names "competitive intensity" as a risk, with "meaningful dry powder" retained to
defend Food leadership "if required" (TLB-014, page 9; TLB-020, page 14).

## Why It Matters
Food leadership is the second half of the capital-allocation decision this plan must resolve, and it is
the smaller-funded half (~55m vs. ~120m for Everyday App) despite defending talabat's largest single
vertical by GMV — Food GMV was USD 6.65bn in FY2025 versus G&R's USD 2.77bn (TLB-002, page 18; TLB-019,
page 14). Understanding why food delivery, still the majority of GMV, receives less than half the
capital of the newer Everyday App initiatives is directly relevant to whether the plan should recommend
rebalancing, and the evidence available (category-share strength, but eroding non-high-value retention
in competitive markets) is genuinely two-sided rather than one-directional.

## Open Questions
- No source discloses how the ~USD 55mn Food-leadership figure is or will be allocated across talabat's 8 markets, or specifically to the "competitive markets" (UAE, Kuwait, Qatar) where the non-high-value retention decline was measured.
- No source quantifies what "meaningful dry powder" (TLB-014, page 9; TLB-020, page 14) actually represents in dollar terms, or under what trigger conditions it would be deployed.
- The corpus's competitive-pressure evidence (non-high-value M1 retention -4% y/y) is explicitly scoped to UAE, Kuwait, and Qatar (TLB-019, page 9) — whether Egypt or other non-GCC markets face comparable Food-leadership pressure is not evidenced anywhere in the corpus.
- No source states why Food, the larger-GMV vertical, receives the smaller capital allocation relative to Everyday App — the corpus documents the split but not management's relative-weighting rationale between the two.

## Business Implications
- Because the competitive-pressure evidence motivating Food leadership is scoped only to UAE, Kuwait, and Qatar, any recommendation to apply Food-leadership-style retention spend to Egypt or other non-GCC markets should be flagged as an extrapolation, not a directly evidenced parallel.
- Management's own stated preference for CVP investment over discounting (TLB-020, page 16) is a directly citable justification for a business plan that similarly recommends AI-personalization/loyalty investment over price competition as the vehicle for allocating Food-leadership capital.
- The disclosed GMV weighting (Food still >70% of Group GMV) against the capital weighting (~55m of 175m, under a third) is a genuine, quantifiable tension the plan should engage with explicitly when proposing any reallocation between the two programme components.
- Because "dry powder" language signals a contingent, not-yet-committed reserve, the plan's allocation recommendation could reasonably propose staged/triggered Food-leadership funding tied to observed competitive-pressure indicators (e.g. non-high-value retention trend) rather than a single upfront figure — consistent with the plan's own stated preference for staged-funding proposals over point estimates.
