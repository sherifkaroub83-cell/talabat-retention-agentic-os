# Pricing

## Overview
The vault has no dedicated Pricing Facts file; pricing evidence must be assembled from Competition.md (commission-rate caps, regulatory pressure) and Revenue.md (take-rate and commission-fee data cited in Retention.md's briefing scope). talabat's revenue model blends commission fees (14.2–16.2% of GMV, FY2025, TLB-019), delivery & service fees, advertising, and subscription fees into a blended take rate of roughly 38–40% of GMV (TLB-020, page 4/10; TLB-001, page 27). Commission pricing is not fully talabat's to set: Qatar imposed a government commission-rate cap and a ban on delivery-registration fees following a Ministry of Commerce and Industry investigation triggered by restaurant-owner complaints (TLB-026, page 46), and Oman has seen licensing disputes (TLB-001, page 32) — both indicating commission pricing is subject to direct regulatory intervention in talabat's markets. Q1 2026 results explicitly note revenue growth outpacing GMV growth partly because of "offsetting lower commission rates" (TLB-014, page 15), implying commission-rate compression is an active, disclosed trend, not merely a risk factor.

## Why It Matters
Pricing sits upstream of retention economics: talabat pro's flat monthly/annual subscription fee, free-delivery threshold, and Partner-funded-savings model are themselves a pricing structure designed to trade a predictable subscription price for higher order frequency and stickiness. Regulatory commission-rate caps (Qatar) and competitive commission undercutting (see [[Competition]]) constrain how much margin talabat can extract from Partners to fund retention investment, which is directly relevant to an Egypt AI-retention strategy that must be financially self-funding rather than assume unconstrained commission headroom.

## Open Questions
- The project's secondary Input_Data corpus (`Input_Data/04_Strategy_News/2025-02-18_talabat_egypt_t_pro_subscription_launch.md`) states talabat pro Egypt launched at EGP 79/month or EGP 799/year with a two-week free trial, and the competitor-landscape file notes elmenus undercuts talabat on restaurant commissions in Egypt — but neither fact has been ingested into the cited `vault/Knowledge/Facts/` or `Sources/` layer, so it cannot be cited here as a vault-grounded fact under this note's sourcing rules. This is a concrete gap: the single most Egypt-relevant, retention-strategy-relevant price point in the whole project (t pro's actual Egypt subscription price) currently exists only outside the frozen knowledge base.
- No source in the vault discloses Egypt-specific commission rates, delivery fees, or take rate — all commission/take-rate figures are group-wide or by GCC/non-GCC aggregate, not Egypt-isolated.
- The vault does not disclose whether Egypt has any commission-rate cap or pricing regulation comparable to Qatar's — Competition.md's Egypt-specific content is limited to relative category-share figures, not pricing regulation.
- It is unclear from the vault how much of the disclosed commission-rate compression (Q1 2026) is attributable to competitive pressure specifically in Egypt versus GCC markets.

## Business Implications
- Because commission pricing is shown to be subject to regulatory intervention (Qatar) and competitive compression (Q1 2026 group-wide trend), the capstone's Egypt retention strategy should not assume talabat can fund AI-driven retention investment purely from expanding commission take rate — subscription and advertising revenue (less exposed to the same caps) are the more defensible funding sources evidenced in the corpus.
- The gap between the vault's cited pricing data and the (uncited) EGP 79/month t pro Egypt price point should be flagged to the user/team and, if the business plan needs the actual Egypt subscription price, that fact should be formally ingested into the vault's Facts/Sources layer (with a proper DocID and citation) rather than referenced informally.
- Given the corpus shows Partner-funded savings rising to an all-time-high share of GMV, an Egypt pricing recommendation could reasonably explore shifting more retention-linked cost from talabat-funded vouchering toward Partner-co-funded pricing mechanisms, mirroring the group-wide trend — though this is an extrapolation, not an Egypt-evidenced conclusion.
