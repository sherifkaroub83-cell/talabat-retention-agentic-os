# 2026 Investment Programme

## Overview
talabat's Board approved a 2026 investment programme totalling **USD 175 million**, split into two named
components: **~USD 120 million for "Everyday App"** initiatives (talabat mart, talabat pro, new
retail/verticals) and **~USD 55 million for "Food leadership"** (retaining medium- and high-value
customers via subscription and targeted incentives, defending against new entrants and incumbents)
(TLB-014, page 8, page 19; TLB-020, page 12, page 16). This is a Group-wide figure — it is not broken
down by country or market anywhere in the corpus. The programme is explicitly funded by a deliberate,
disclosed margin step-down: Adjusted EBITDA margin guided from 6.0% (Q4 2025 baseline) to a ~4.6%
FY2026 mid-range, of which the Everyday App and Food-leadership investments account for roughly 0.7pp
and 0.5pp of GMV respectively (TLB-014, page 6, page 19).

## Why It Matters
This is the single decision the capstone's business plan exists to answer: how should talabat allocate
this USD 175 million across Everyday App and Food-leadership initiatives, and — within them — across
markets and initiative categories, given that talabat itself has disclosed the Group-level split but not
a market-level or initiative-level one (see `Problem_Charter.md`). Every other note in this set of five
(Everyday App, Food Leadership, Multi-Verticality, Grocery and Retail) is a component or a supporting
mechanism of this one programme. Getting the programme's own disclosure history precisely right — what
was said when, and at what level of aggregation — is a precondition for not overstating what the
corpus supports.

## How the disclosed figures evolved across the corpus (traced to primary sources only)
The full USD 175mn = ~120mn + ~55mn breakdown is **not** present in every document that discusses the
programme; earlier and same-period documents disclose partial or differently-scoped figures. Tracing
strictly document-by-document:
- **13 February 2026 (TLB-019, Q4/FY2025 Results Presentation):** discloses the **USD 175mn Board-approved total** explicitly ("part of a $175mn Board-approved programme," page 11) and states ">$100mn of investment in 2026" is aimed at "doubling down on building talabat as the everyday app" — but does not yet use the term "Food leadership" or give a precise USD 120mn Everyday App figure in this document.
- **13 February 2026, same date (per the Q-Commerce and Talabat Pro Facts files, TLB-009 Q4/FY2025 Results Press Release):** discloses ">USD 100 million" allocated across two named priority areas — scaling talabat mart and strengthening talabat pro — without naming a "Food leadership" component or a USD 175mn total in the bullets captured from that document.
- **12 May 2026 (TLB-011, Q1 2026 Results Press Release):** sharpens the Everyday App figure to a precise **USD 120 million**, now spanning three named areas (talabat mart, talabat pro, and "new offerings in the retail space"), with **~USD 25 million deployed during Q1 2026** — but this press release's captured disclosure does not itself name the USD 55mn Food-leadership component.
- **12 May 2026 (TLB-020, Q1 2026 Results Presentation) and 14 May 2026 (TLB-014, Investor Presentation):** both give the **full reconciled breakdown** — USD 175mn total = ~USD 120mn Everyday App (further split ~USD 75mn opex / ~USD 45mn capex) + ~USD 55mn Food leadership — together with the realized and guided Adjusted EBITDA margin bridges quantifying each component's impact (TLB-020, page 8, page 12, page 16; TLB-014, page 6, page 8, page 19).

**Conclusion supported by the evidence:** the USD 175 million total and its two named components are
consistently disclosed from May 2026 onward (TLB-014, TLB-020); the total itself is evidenced as early
as February 2026 (TLB-019), but the precise USD 120mn/USD 55mn split by name only appears in the two
most recent (May 2026) investor-facing documents in the corpus. This is a genuine sharpening of
disclosure over time, not an unreconciled contradiction — but the plan should still cite the May 2026
documents (TLB-014, TLB-020) as the source of the exact split, not earlier ones.

## Open Questions
- No source discloses how the USD 175mn (or either sub-component) is or will be allocated across talabat's 8 markets — this is the exact gap the capstone's plan is built to address with a ranged, staged recommendation rather than a false-precision figure.
- No source discloses an aggregate or per-component expected return (ROI, payback period, or targeted GMV/EBITDA uplift) for the programme.
- The corpus does not explain why the earlier (February 2026) disclosure named only two Everyday App areas (talabat mart, talabat pro) while the later (May 2026) disclosure names three (adding "new retail offerings") — whether this reflects a genuinely new initiative or a re-labeling is not stated.
- It is not stated whether the ~USD 25mn deployed in Q1 2026 (TLB-011) was drawn proportionally from the Everyday App and Food-leadership components, or from Everyday App alone.

## Business Implications
- This programme, and its Group-level disclosure only, is the evidentiary reason the capstone's plan must express market-level allocation recommendations as ranges and staged-funding proposals rather than precise figures (per `Problem_Charter.md`'s own evidence-limitations section).
- The consistent 2.2:1 weighting toward Everyday App over Food leadership is itself a disclosed management judgment worth engaging with directly: any recommendation to alter this ratio should state why the existing evidence does or does not support a different weighting.
- The margin step-down funding mechanism (6.0%→~4.6% guided) means the plan's financial projections must treat 2026 as a below-trend margin year by design, not as a signal of underlying deterioration — consistent with TLB-020's own framing ("investments rather than costs," page 16).
- Because the corpus's own investment-figure disclosure sharpened materially between February and May 2026, the plan should prefer the most recent (May 2026) sources for any dollar-figure citation of this programme, and should flag explicitly if it is citing an earlier, less granular figure.
