# Customer Retention Drivers

## Thesis
Ranked by the strength and specificity of talabat's own evidence, five retention mechanisms actually work in the corpus — talabat pro subscription (dominant and most measured), multi-vertical engagement (the largest single behavioral delta, and pro's amplifier), talabat Rewards and PostPaid (secondary but real, product-embedded levers), and Family Plan (narrow but high-uplift) — with AI/personalization functioning not as a sixth item on the list but as the infrastructure that targets and times all five. This ranking is this note's own synthesis: the corpus states each uplift figure individually but never ranks them against one another.

## Synthesis
**1. talabat pro is the most directly and repeatedly quantified retention lever in the entire corpus.** [[Talabat Pro]] and Facts/Talabat_Pro.md document a consistent order-frequency uplift (20-28% across multiple periods and documents — TLB-023, page 8 cites "over 20%"; TLB-001, page 18 cites 28%) and a directly measured 12-month retention uplift versus lookalike non-subscribers that ranges from 26% (TLB-019, page 11) to 32% (TLB-015, page 78, page 104) depending on cohort and measurement period. The single richest figure in the whole corpus is talabat pro's **136% gross-profit-per-customer uplift** in the 30 days following subscription versus the 30 days before (TLB-015, page 78, page 104) — a direct, causally-framed link from subscription to unit economics that [[Customer Lifetime Value]] treats as the corpus's clearest LTV signal. By Q1 2026, pro subscribers represented roughly half of platform GMV (TLB-020, page 8), and pro is explicitly named as the vehicle for the 2026 board-level objective to "retain high and medium value customers against partial or complete churn to competition" (TLB-002, page 14). Crucially, pro's effect is not flat — it compounds with the next driver.

**2. Multi-vertical engagement is the largest single behavioral delta disclosed anywhere in the corpus, and it amplifies pro's effect rather than operating independently of it.** [[Customer Journey]] and Facts/Retention.md show mono-vertical subscribers deliver a +16 percentage-point M1 retention lift versus mono-vertical non-subscribers, while multi-vertical subscribers deliver +20 percentage points (TLB-019, page 10) — i.e., the same subscription product performs measurably better once a customer is also ordering across verticals. The underlying frequency gap is stark: food-only customers order 3.8 times per month versus 13.0 times per month for multi-vertical customers (TLB-012, page 7) — roughly 3.4x, a larger proportional gap than any single-product uplift figure in the corpus, though it is worth flagging that this comparison and pro's uplift are not measured on strictly the same basis and the two effects are not disclosed as separable (a multi-vertical pro subscriber's frequency cannot be decomposed into "how much is pro, how much is multi-verticality" from the corpus alone). By December 2025, 73% of GMV came from multi-vertical customers, and that share grew a further 4 percentage points YoY into 2026 (TLB-019; TLB-014, page 14) — this is a customer population that is both large and growing, not a niche.

**3. talabat Rewards is the corpus's clearest direct anti-churn claim, distinct from pro.** [[Promotions]] and Facts/Retention.md cite talabat Rewards as delivering a >15% order-frequency increase within 30 days of first redemption, at an 18% adoption rate (TLB-026, page 134), and — uniquely among the corpus's retention products — talabat's own language describes Rewards as "encouraging consistent behaviour, increasing loyalty and **reducing churn**" (TLB-015, page 79). No other product in the vault is linked to churn reduction in such direct language. The caveat, noted in [[Promotions]]'s Open Questions, is that it is unclear whether this uplift is causal (the reward drove the behavior) or reflects self-selection (already-engaged customers redeem first) — the corpus does not resolve this.

**4. talabat PostPaid (BNPL) is a smaller but real, product-embedded lever.** Per [[Relationship_Map]] §5 and Facts/Retention.md, PostPaid is described as "accretive to order frequency and customer retention," with a measured 14% frequency increase post-adoption (TLB-001, page 9, page 18). It sits in the same "embedded FinTech" category as the ADCB co-branded credit card, which "strengthened customer retention and engagement" via cashback and free delivery (TLB-001, page 9, page 13).

**5. Family Plan shows the single highest uplift number in the corpus, but on the narrowest evidence base.** Per [[Talabat Pro]] and Facts/Retention.md, four-member Family Plan subscribers show a >60% retention premium over solo-plan subscribers (TLB-018, page 6) — numerically the largest uplift figure anywhere in the vault, but disclosed in a single document, for a single household-size segment, with no adoption-rate or country breakout. It should be weighted accordingly: a real, evidenced signal, not a headline statistic to lead with.

**AI/personalization is the infrastructure underneath all five, not a sixth parallel lever.** [[Personalization]] and [[AI]] establish that talabat's machine-learning models require roughly six orders to build a usable customer profile, after which they decide when to surface a talabat pro offer, a Rewards redemption, or an item-level recommendation (TLB-001, page 22-23; TLB-002, page 15; TLB-023, page 8). The estimated EBITDA contribution of this layer rose from USD 14mn+ (FY2024) to USD 30mn+ (FY2025) (TLB-001, page 23; TLB-002, page 16). This is why the ranking above should not be read as five independent levers competing for investment — personalization is the targeting and timing mechanism that makes pro, Rewards, PostPaid, and the Family Plan land with the right customer at the right moment, and its own EBITDA impact figure is best read as a multiplier on the other four rather than a comparable, standalone uplift statistic. Management's own stated preference reinforces the ranking's logic: the ~USD 55mn "Food leadership" investment is explicitly framed as investing in customer value proposition (CVP) — the product-embedded mechanisms above — "rather than matching competitor discounts/vouchers" (TLB-020, page 16), i.e., talabat's own capital-allocation choice favors exactly the ranked, product-embedded levers this note identifies over blanket promotional spend.

## Evidence Basis

- [[Customer Journey]] and Facts/Retention.md — the mono- vs. multi-vertical M1 retention delta (+16pp/+20pp), the 3.8x/13.0x frequency comparison, and the multi-vertical GMV-share figures
- [[Promotions]] — talabat Rewards' adoption rate, frequency uplift, and "reducing churn" language, plus PostPaid and ADCB card framing
- [[Relationship_Map]] §5 — PostPaid's 14% frequency-increase figure
- [[Personalization]] and [[AI]] — the 6-order threshold and the USD 14mn→30mn+ EBITDA impact of the targeting/timing layer

- [[Customer Lifetime Value]] — the LTV interpretation of pro's gross-profit uplift figure

## Egypt-Specific Considerations
This is the single most important caveat in this note: **none of the uplift figures above are confirmed for Egypt.** [[Talabat Pro]]'s Open Questions state explicitly that the corpus's headline adoption and uplift figures (2.1x adoption growth, 28% frequency uplift, 32% retention uplift) are measured on the six GCC/Jordan markets live before December 2024 and **exclude Egypt and Iraq** by name (TLB-012, page 17; TLB-013, page 8; TLB-016, page 9) — talabat pro only launched in Egypt in February 2025. The multi-vertical frequency and M1-retention-delta figures are not broken out by country anywhere in the corpus, so whether Egypt customers show the same 3.8x/13.0x gap or the same +16pp/+20pp multi-vertical amplification is unverified. Rewards, PostPaid, and Family Plan adoption and uplift data are similarly undisclosed for Egypt specifically. The ranked argument in this note should therefore be read as **the group-wide "what works" playbook, imported into Egypt as a working hypothesis** — not as an Egypt-validated ranking. Given Egypt's status as talabat's largest user base and third-largest GMV market with talabat pro live for only about a year at the time of the most recent corpus documents (per [[Egypt]]), this is precisely the "large base, early loyalty penetration" profile where the ranked playbook has the most theoretical room to compound — but that is this note's synthesis, not a disclosed Egypt outcome.

## Open Questions
- No Egypt-specific figure exists for any of the five ranked drivers (pro, multi-vertical, Rewards, PostPaid, Family Plan) — all are Group-level, GCC-cohort-based, or explicitly Egypt-excluded.
- The corpus does not disclose whether talabat pro's uplift figures and the multi-vertical M1-retention delta are additive, overlapping, or interacting in ways that would change this note's ranking if decomposed.
- Rewards' >15% frequency uplift is not distinguished as causal versus self-selection anywhere in the corpus.
- No figure attributes any share of the AI/personalization EBITDA estimate (USD 14mn→30mn+) specifically to its role in timing pro, Rewards, or PostPaid offers versus its other applications (logistics, ad-targeting) — the multiplier framing above is this note's inference from how the mechanism is described, not a disclosed decomposition.

## Strategic Implications
- The business plan's Egypt retention recommendation should prioritize talabat pro conversion and multi-vertical (food-to-grocery) cross-sell as the top two interventions, since these carry the strongest and most consistently repeated evidence base in the corpus, even though neither is Egypt-confirmed.
- Rewards, PostPaid, and Family Plan should be positioned as a secondary, complementary layer — real, evidenced mechanisms, but with thinner and more caveated support than pro and multi-verticality.
- AI/personalization investment should be framed in the business plan as necessary enabling infrastructure for the other four levers to work at all (targeting, timing), not as an optional or parallel initiative competing with them for budget.
- Any specific uplift percentage used in the business plan's Egypt-facing financial model (e.g., "assume X% frequency uplift from pro in Egypt") must be explicitly labeled as an extrapolated Group-level figure, not a confirmed Egypt statistic, consistent with the project's standing instruction on synthetic/derived data.
- Because the corpus never ranks these drivers against each other, this note's prioritization is itself a claim the business plan is making on the capstone's authority — it should be presented in the deliverable as the plan's own analytical judgment, sourced from but not dictated by the corpus.
