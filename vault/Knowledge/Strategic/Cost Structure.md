# Cost Structure

## Thesis
talabat's disclosed cost structure is thin at the line-item level — the corpus gives only three real cost
categories (delivery costs, order processing & handling costs, other direct costs, all inside "cost of
sales") plus one internally-defined retention-specific cost metric (CARC) — but even this limited detail is
enough to show a consistent pattern: delivery cost is the largest, most stable cost driver as a share of GMV,
while the "other direct costs" and CARC lines are the ones rising fastest, both linked in the corpus to the
same underlying cause — the mix shift toward Groceries & Retail/quick commerce and toward retention-oriented
customer investment. Margin structure is therefore better read as a mix-driven story than a pure-efficiency
story.

## Synthesis
The corpus's fullest cost-of-sales breakdown is FY2025 pro forma: Cost of sales totaled USD 2,632mn, made up
of delivery costs USD 1,457mn (+28% y/y), order processing and handling costs USD 180mn (+30% y/y), and
other direct costs USD 995mn (+47% y/y) (TLB-002, page 20). Delivery costs are consistently the largest
component and are reported net at 6.3% of GMV in FY2025, up from 6.0% in FY2024 (TLB-002, page 20) — a
modest but directionally rising cost-intensity trend. "Other direct costs" is the fastest-growing line by
percentage (+47%), which — read alongside [[Quick Commerce]]'s finding that tMart's GMV share and near-95%
take rate are rising, and [[Revenue Drivers]]'s explicit flag that grocery/G&R mix dilutes take rate and
margin — is most plausibly linked to talabat's principal-model (owned-inventory) Q-Commerce business, where
talabat bears the cost of goods in a way it does not in the agent-model Food marketplace. The corpus does not
state this link directly; it is this note's synthesis of two facts that are each individually cited but not
connected by any single source.

Layered on top of cost of sales is Customer Acquisition and Retention Costs (CARC), an internally-defined
metric distinct from IFRS cost-of-sales categories, which the corpus tracks explicitly because it is the
cost side of the retention thesis this capstone is built around. CARC rose from USD 89mn (1.5% of GMV,
2023) to USD 103mn (1.4% of GMV, 2024) to USD 155mn (1.6% of GMV, 2025) (TLB-001, page 28; TLB-002, page 21).
Its composition also shifted: the 2024 figure combined Customer Acquisition Costs of USD 44mn with
talabat-funded vouchering of USD 59mn, while the 2025 figure combined talabat-funded vouchering of USD 89mn
with customer marketing costs of USD 67mn (TLB-001, page 28; TLB-002, page 21) — vouchering roughly
doubled or more year-over-year (fully replacing what CAC previously represented in the internal taxonomy),
even as CARC as a whole rose only modestly as a share of GMV. [[Promotions]] frames this same data as
evidence that talabat distinguishes structured, product-embedded retention tools (Rewards, PostPaid,
Family Plan — each with a disclosed frequency-uplift figure) from blanket vouchering, which is tracked
purely as a cost line rather than a loyalty investment. For a cost-structure lens, the takeaway is that the
cost of retaining and acquiring customers is rising in absolute terms and as a modest share of GMV — meaning
any AI-driven retention strategy the capstone proposes is not being layered onto a flat or falling cost
base, and its business case should explicitly argue for *efficiency* within CARC (shifting spend from
generic vouchering toward AI-targeted, personalized incentives) rather than simply assuming additional spend
is costless.

Margin structure across the corpus shows profitability growing in absolute dollars but not always expanding
as a percentage of GMV: Gross profit margin was 12.3% of GMV in FY2024 and 11.9% in FY2025 (pro forma;
Facts/Marketplace.md; TLB-002, page 20) — a slight *decline* despite gross profit dollars rising 23% — and
Adjusted EBITDA margin similarly slipped from 6.7% (FY2024) to 6.5% (FY2025) even as Adjusted EBITDA grew
24% ([[Profitability]]). This is the clearest quantified evidence in the corpus that talabat's overall cost
structure is not simply scaling down with volume (which would show margin *expansion*) — it is being
actively diluted by something growing faster than the core Food marketplace, which every note that touches
this topic ([[Quick Commerce]], [[Revenue Drivers]], [[Profitability]]) attributes to the G&R/tMart mix
shift, explicitly flagged by management itself as an FY2026 guidance headwind alongside "increased customer
incentives" (TLB-014, page 15) — directly tying the cost-structure story back to CARC and retention spend.

Egypt is the one place in the corpus where a genuine, audited, segment-level cost figure exists rather than
a Group-level aggregate: the Egypt segment's FY2025 audited financials disclose a delivery cost of
USD 113.13mn and D&A of USD 6.21mn against Egypt segment revenue of USD 509.94mn and gross profit of
USD 124.996mn (TLB-002, page 111; Facts/Marketplace.md). This means Egypt's delivery cost ran at roughly
22% of Egypt segment revenue in FY2025 — a figure the business plan can cite directly, though it cannot be
directly compared to the Group's 6.3%-of-GMV delivery-cost ratio because the denominators differ (segment
revenue vs. Group GMV) and Egypt's own GMV is not disclosed (see [[Segment Reporting]] and [[GMV]] Open
Questions).

## Evidence Basis
- `Facts/Marketplace.md` — the primary source for cost-of-sales breakdown (delivery, order processing/handling, other direct costs), gross profit, and Adjusted EBITDA margin figures.
- [[Profitability]] — the margin-compression-despite-dollar-growth pattern (gross margin 12.3%→11.9%, EBITDA margin 6.7%→6.5%) and the Egypt segment profit figures.
- [[Revenue Drivers]] — the explicit link between G&R/tMart mix shift and margin dilution.
- [[Quick Commerce]] — tMart's principal (owned-inventory) model and near-95% take rate, the basis for this note's synthesis linking "other direct costs" growth to the Q-Commerce mix shift.
- [[Promotions]] — the CARC composition detail (vouchering vs. customer marketing/CAC) and the distinction between structured retention products and blanket discounting.
- `Facts/Retention.md` and `Facts/Customer_Growth.md` — CARC and CAC figures by year, with citations.
- [[Segment Reporting]] and [[Egypt]] — context for why Egypt's delivery-cost figure is disclosed at all (the FY2025 IFRS 8 segment-reporting change) and its limits.

## Egypt-Specific Considerations
Egypt's FY2025 audited delivery cost (USD 113.13mn) and D&A (USD 6.21mn) are genuine, citable Egypt-specific
cost figures — a rarity in this corpus. However, this is the *only* granular Egypt cost detail available:
there is no Egypt-specific CARC, no Egypt order-processing/handling cost, no Egypt "other direct costs"
line, and no Egypt Adjusted EBITDA margin (only revenue, gross profit, profit before tax, and net profit are
disclosed at the Egypt-segment level, per [[Profitability]] Open Questions). The business plan should treat
the Egypt delivery-cost figure as a genuine anchor point but should not assume Egypt's broader cost
structure (CARC intensity, mix-shift dilution) mirrors the Group average — Egypt's cost base may behave
differently given its much younger talabat pro programme (live since Feb 2025 only) and undisclosed
quick-commerce penetration there.

## Open Questions
- The corpus does not disclose granular cost-line detail (delivery cost, order processing, other direct
  costs) broken out by segment or country anywhere except the one Egypt figure above — the "thin disclosure"
  flagged in this note's thesis is a genuine corpus limitation, not a gap in this synthesis.
- The link this note draws between "other direct costs" (+47% y/y) and the Q-Commerce/G&R mix shift is
  inferred by connecting two separately-cited facts; no single source in the corpus states this causal link
  explicitly.
- CARC's internal composition changed its labeling between 2024 ("Customer Acquisition Costs") and 2025
  ("customer marketing costs") without the corpus explaining whether this is a relabeling of the same
  category or a genuine definitional change.
- No document states an Egypt-specific CARC or customer-acquisition-cost figure, so it is not possible to
  assess whether Egypt's retention-spend efficiency differs from the Group average.

## Strategic Implications
- Because CARC (the retention-relevant cost line) is rising as a share of GMV, the AI-driven retention
  business plan should explicitly position AI-targeted personalization as a way to improve CARC
  *efficiency* — shifting the mix within CARC from blanket vouchering toward higher-return, product-embedded
  incentives — rather than only asking for incremental spend.
- The disclosed Egypt delivery-cost figure (USD 113.13mn, FY2025) gives the business plan a real, citable
  cost anchor for at least one line of the P&L, which should be used explicitly rather than substituting a
  Group-level proxy where a genuine Egypt figure exists.
- The margin-dilution pattern linked to G&R/quick-commerce mix shift means any Egypt retention strategy that
  encourages cross-selling into grocery/quick commerce should model the margin trade-off explicitly, not
  present multi-vertical engagement as costless upside.
- Given how little granular Egypt cost data exists beyond the one disclosed delivery-cost figure, the
  business plan should flag Egypt-specific cost-line disclosure (CARC, order-processing cost, quick-commerce
  cost of goods) as a data gap worth recommending talabat instrument and report going forward — mirroring
  the same recommendation [[AI]] makes for AI deployment data.
