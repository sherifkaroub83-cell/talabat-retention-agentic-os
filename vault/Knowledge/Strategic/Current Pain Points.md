# Current Pain Points

## Thesis
This note is explicitly a roll-up of gaps, not a discovered set of operational problems: nearly every "pain point" available to the capstone is an absence of disclosure — no churn rate, no Egypt-specific AI or personalisation data, no absolute LTV figure, thin AdTech penetration data, heavy third-party rider dependency, Egypt FX/macro exposure, and two unexplained senior personnel changes — scattered across the "Open Questions" and "Limitations" sections of the Topic Notes and Facts/Entities files. Naming them together here is itself the contribution: individually each is a footnote, but collectively they define the evidentiary boundary the AI-driven retention business plan must work within and around.

## Synthesis
The single largest pain point, and the one the whole capstone exists to work around, is that **no churn rate is disclosed anywhere in the corpus** — for talabat overall or for Egypt specifically. [[Customer Churn]] states this directly: every "churn" reference in the 29 documents is qualitative or inferred from a retention statement (e.g., the 2026 objective to defend against "partial or complete churn to competition," TLB-002, page 14, or the Qatar closure recovery narrative), never a measured figure. The goodwill-impairment cohort models in TLB-004 and TLB-008 reference a "customer retention/reorder rate" input but never disclose its value — meaning talabat plausibly does model churn/reorder internally, but does not publish it. This is compounded by [[Customer Lifetime Value]]'s parallel gap: no absolute LTV figure, in any currency, for any segment or country, exists anywhere in the corpus — every LTV-adjacent figure (the 3.5x-2.0x cohort spend multiples, the 136% gross-profit-per-customer uplift from talabat pro) is relative, and none is Egypt-specific.

Egypt compounds these Group-level gaps with its own layer of absence. [[Egypt]]'s Open Questions catalogue the pattern precisely: no Egypt-specific talabat pro adoption rate, frequency uplift, or retention uplift exists (all uplift metrics — 28% frequency, 26-32% retention — are Group-level or explicitly exclude Egypt at the time they were measured); no Egypt-specific CAC, CARC, or LTV figure exists; no Egypt Partner, restaurant, or rider count is broken out from platform-wide totals; and the corpus's own category-share figures for Egypt don't reconcile (10x+ in TLB-001/TLB-002 versus 1x+ rising to 4x+ in TLB-014), a discrepancy the vault never explains. [[AI]] and [[Personalization]] add the AI-specific version of this same gap: no document discloses whether Egypt runs the same AI/ML models as the GCC or a less mature version, and none of the USD 14mn→30mn+ personalisation EBITDA figures is broken out by country. [[Advertising]] shows AdTech has essentially no Egypt visibility either — no Egypt-specific AdTech revenue, penetration, or ROAS figure exists, even though the 7%-of-GMV benchmark has already been reached for talabat mart in the UAE, implying real headroom that cannot currently be sized for Egypt.

Two further pain points are operational rather than data gaps, but are similarly under-examined for Egypt specifically. [[Riders]] documents that ~90-95% of talabat's riders operate via third-party logistics (3PL) or freelance arrangements rather than direct employment, flagged by talabat itself as a labour-compliance and service-quality-control risk (TLB-001, page 34, 47; TLB-026, page 138) — and no Egypt-specific rider count, tenure, or attrition figure exists to assess whether this dependency is more or less acute in Egypt than elsewhere (Egypt's only rider-specific data point is a narrow 200-EV pilot, too thin to generalise from). Separately, though not a topic note in this batch, Egypt's currency exposure is a structural feature of the corpus's own segment-reporting logic: Egypt's FY2025 swing to profitability and its ~81% YoY Q1 2026 growth (per [[Egypt]] and [[Relationship_Map]] §7) are reported in USD, meaning EGP-denominated local results are subject to translation effects the corpus does not decompose — a real, if implicit, macro pain point for financially modelling Egypt-specific retention ROI.

Finally, [[Corporate Structure]] and [[Executives|Entities/Executives]] contribute a governance-stability pain point, covered in depth in [[Decision-Making Process]]: a CEO transition (Rodriguez → Gyssels) and a board-seat change (Al Jbori → Al-Halabi) both occur within the corpus's timeline without any source narrating why. This is not a customer-facing pain point, but it is a real one for a business plan asking for multi-year executive sponsorship of an Egypt retention programme.

## Egypt-Specific Considerations
This entire note is, by construction, about Egypt-specific absence layered on top of Group-level thinness. Almost none of the pain points listed here are Egypt-*confirmed* problems (e.g., there is no source stating "Egypt's churn rate is high" or "Egypt's rider attrition is a problem") — they are gaps in what can be *known* about Egypt from this corpus. The one clearly Egypt-specific and directly evidenced fact among all of this is that talabat pro has been live in Egypt only since February 2025, per [[Egypt]] — meaning Egypt's retention infrastructure genuinely is young, not merely under-disclosed. The business plan should keep this distinction sharp: "young programme, no reporting yet" is a defensible reading of the evidence; "known operational failure" is not.

## Open Questions
- Whether talabat's internal cohort models (which do reference a retention/reorder-rate input) are ever used to compute or track an Egypt-specific figure — the corpus gives no indication either way.
- Whether the two Egypt category-share figures (10x+ vs. 1x+→4x+) reflect a real trend or a methodology artefact — unresolved in the corpus.
- Whether Egypt's FX exposure is hedged, and to what extent EGP volatility affects the USD-reported Egypt segment figures — not addressed by any source read for this project.
- Whether the CEO and board-seat changes have any bearing on the continuity of the 2026 Everyday App investment programme or the Egypt-specific portion of it.

## Strategic Implications
- The business plan should treat this note as its evidence-gap appendix: every synthetic or estimated Egypt figure used in the financial modelling sections should trace back to one of the gaps catalogued here, per the project's standing instruction that synthetic data must be clearly labeled and never blended with disclosed company data.
- The absence of a churn baseline is the single strongest argument for the plan to propose an Egypt-specific churn-measurement and prediction capability as a first-order recommendation, not an afterthought (developed further in [[Future AI Opportunities]]).
- The 3PL/freelance rider-dependency risk should be named explicitly in the plan's risk section wherever a recommendation assumes consistent delivery-experience quality as a retention precondition.
- The governance-stability gap (unexplained CEO/board turnover) should be flagged as an execution risk for any multi-year Egypt programme, independent of the strategy's merits.
