# Decision-Making Process

## Thesis
The corpus reveals talabat's decision-*outputs* — who governs, what gets funded, and where multi-year commitments get announced — far more clearly than its decision-*mechanics*; governance is visibly concentrated in Delivery Hero SE's own officers, and strategic bets are framed and communicated through a recognisable venue pattern (Capital Markets Day, Annual Report, quarterly results), but how an investment case actually gets evaluated and approved internally is a real, largely unfilled gap that any Egypt-specific retention business case will need to navigate blind.

## Synthesis
What the corpus shows clearly is *structure*, via [[Corporate Structure]]: Talabat Holding PLC's Board is chaired by Pieter-Jan Vandepitte, who is simultaneously Delivery Hero SE's COO, with the Vice-Chairperson and a further Non-Executive Director also holding concurrent Delivery Hero SE C-suite roles (General Counsel, interim CFO). This is not merely an affiliated-company relationship — it is the same small set of people sitting on both the parent's executive team and the Egypt-operating subsidiary's ultimate governing board. Any Egypt retention investment case will, by this structural fact alone, be evaluated by decision-makers whose primary institutional seat is at the Delivery Hero Group level, not the Talabat Holding PLC or Egypt level specifically.

What the corpus shows about *how strategic bets get framed* is the venue pattern documented across [[Investor Relations]] and [[Growth Strategy]]: multi-year capital commitments are announced at set moments — the Capital Markets Day (TLB-015) is where medium-term targets and roadmap items (including the AI items catalogued in [[AI Opportunities]]) get set out; the Annual Report is where the following year's named strategic objective gets stated in prose (e.g., the 2026 objective to "retain high and medium value customers against partial or complete churn to competition," TLB-002, page 14); and quarterly results presentations (TLB-019, TLB-020) are where specific dollar figures attach to a strategy already announced in principle — the ~USD 100-120mn "Everyday App" 2026 investment programme, split roughly USD 75mn opex / USD 45mn capex (TLB-020, page 12, 16), is presented as an already-decided capital allocation, not a proposal under evaluation. This is a management-to-investor communication cadence, not a description of the internal deliberation that produced the number — the corpus shows the announcement, never the meeting, memo, or business case behind it.

What the corpus does *not* show, and this is the load-bearing gap for this note, is any internal decision-making mechanism: no document describes a capital-allocation committee, an investment-approval threshold, a stage-gate process, or how a country-level initiative (an Egypt retention programme, specifically) would move from proposal to funded pillar. [[Corporate Structure]]'s Open Questions section states this directly: the vault does not show whether Egypt's two legal entities (Delivery Hero Egypt SAE, Delivery Hero Dmart Egypt LLC) have any local board representation, advisory committee, or decision rights distinct from the Talabat Holding PLC board — meaning it is not established from the corpus whether an Egypt retention initiative would be approved locally or only at Group level.

Layered onto this structural opacity are two personnel changes the corpus records but never explains, both flagged in [[Corporate Structure]] and [[Executives|Entities/Executives]]: a CEO transition from Tomaso Rodriguez to Toon Gyssels sometime between the Q2 2025 results (TLB-018, Aug 2025) and the Q4/FY2025 results (TLB-009, Feb 2026) — with TLB-008 (same reporting period as TLB-009) still listing Rodriguez as "Executive Director," suggesting an internal move rather than a departure, though no source confirms this — and a board-seat change from Muhammad Hussain Ghati Al Jbori to Abdul Wahab Al-Halabi between TLB-004/TLB-026 and TLB-008. Neither change is narrated anywhere in the 29-document corpus. For a decision-making-process note, this matters directly: continuity of the people who would sponsor or approve a multi-year Egypt retention programme cannot be assumed from the available evidence, since the two most senior decision-making seats visible in the corpus (CEO, one board seat) have both turned over recently without public explanation.

## Evidence Basis
- [[Corporate Structure]] — board composition, the Delivery Hero SE officer overlap, and both open governance questions (Egypt-entity decision rights; the unexplained personnel changes).
- [[Executives|Entities/Executives]] — the primary record of the Rodriguez→Gyssels CEO transition and the Al Jbori→Al-Halabi board change, with the corpus's own caveat that neither is narrated by any source.
- [[Investor Relations]] — the recurring disclosure cadence (Capital Markets Day, Annual Report, quarterly results) as the venue pattern through which strategic decisions become visible externally.
- [[Growth Strategy]] — the 2026 Everyday App investment programme as an example of a strategic bet presented at the announcement stage, with no visible internal approval trail.
- [[AI Opportunities]] — the specific AI roadmap items announced via this same venue pattern (Capital Markets Day roadmap language).

## Egypt-Specific Considerations
Egypt has no visible decision-making apparatus of its own in the corpus. [[Corporate Structure]] confirms Egypt operates through two dedicated legal entities (Delivery Hero Egypt SAE, Delivery Hero Dmart Egypt LLC) under services agreements with Delivery Hero, but the corpus never states whether either entity has a local board, an advisory committee, or delegated approval authority. Every strategic decision visible in the corpus — the 2026 objective, the Everyday App investment, the AI roadmap items — is announced at the Group or Talabat Holding PLC level with no country-level breakout of how or whether Egypt's case was made separately. This is a genuine structural unknown, not an extrapolation: the business plan cannot assume Egypt has (or lacks) a local decision-making seat.

## Open Questions
- No document describes an internal capital-allocation process, investment committee, or approval threshold for country-level strategic initiatives.
- Whether Egypt's two legal entities have any local governance body with delegated decision rights is not stated anywhere in the corpus.
- Neither the CEO transition (Rodriguez → Gyssels) nor the board-seat change (Al Jbori → Al-Halabi) is explained by any of the 29 source documents — both are observed, not narrated.
- Whether Rodriguez's continued listing as "Executive Director" in TLB-008 reflects an internal move or a coincidental title overlap is not confirmed by any source.
- The corpus does not indicate how frequently, or through what internal process, the Board revisits committed multi-year programmes like the Everyday App investment once announced.

## Strategic Implications
- The business plan's implementation section should explicitly name Delivery Hero Egypt SAE and Delivery Hero Dmart Egypt LLC as the operating vehicles for any recommendation, per [[Corporate Structure]], rather than assuming an abstract "Egypt business unit" with its own authority.
- Because governance is visibly Group-concentrated, the capstone's investment ask should be framed to satisfy Group-level criteria (consistent with the Delivery Hero-aligned Board) rather than assuming a country-level approval path exists.
- The unexplained CEO and board-seat changes belong in the business plan's risk section as a leadership-continuity caveat: a multi-year Egypt retention programme's sponsorship cannot be assumed stable given two recent, unexplained turnovers at the most senior visible decision-making seats.
- Given how thin the actual decision-mechanics evidence is, the plan should be explicit that its "how this gets approved" section is a reasoned proposal built on the visible governance structure (Board composition, IR cadence, the Everyday App precedent), not a description of a documented talabat approval process — because no such process is disclosed in the corpus.
