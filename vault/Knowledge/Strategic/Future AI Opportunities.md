# Future AI Opportunities

## Thesis
The evidence gaps identified across [[Customer Churn]], [[AI]], [[Personalization]], and [[Egypt]] — read together with Egypt's newly-standalone, newly-profitable segment status and talabat's own stated ambition to extend AI-driven personalisation and targeting — suggest an opportunity for the business plan to propose Egypt-specific, AI-driven churn prediction and early-tenure retention intervention as a new initiative. **This note is explicitly the capstone's own recommendation-space synthesis, not a report of talabat's plans.** Nothing below should be read as "talabat plans to do X" — it is "the gaps below suggest the business plan could propose X," and the distinction is deliberate and load-bearing throughout.

## Synthesis
Four separate, independently-evidenced facts combine to create the opportunity this note argues for — none of them, individually, states or implies the recommendation; the recommendation is this capstone's construction from connecting them.

First, [[Customer Churn]] establishes that talabat discloses no churn rate, churn definition, or churn cohort analysis anywhere in the corpus, for the Group or for Egypt — the only concrete anchor is the 2026 objective to "retain high and medium value customers against partial or complete churn to competition" (TLB-002, page 14), a named management concern with no attached measurement. Second, [[Personalization]] and [[Recommendation Systems]] establish that talabat's own personalisation engine requires approximately six orders before it activates, and that pre-personalisation customers, by inference (not a disclosed figure), plausibly carry elevated churn risk during that unpersonalised window — the corpus states the threshold and the aggregate EBITDA impact of personalisation, but never a segmented before/after retention comparison. Third, [[Egypt]] establishes that Egypt only became a standalone, IFRS 8-qualifying reportable segment with FY2025 results, swinging from near-breakeven (Q1 2025 profit before tax USD 0.5m) to solidly profitable (Q1 2026 USD 9.4m) within a year, with talabat pro live in Egypt for barely a year at the time of the most recent results (launched February 2025) — meaning Egypt is simultaneously the Group's largest user base, its newest profitable segment, and its least mature loyalty market. Fourth, [[AI Opportunities]] establishes that talabat has itself already named forward AI ambitions — new-form-factor recommendations, cross-sell timing/incentive optimisation, and deepened adtech-embedded personalisation — as roadmap items, without attaching any of them to a specific market or timeline.

Put together, these four facts do not describe a talabat plan for Egypt — they describe a **vacuum with a shape**: a market that has just become large and profitable enough to matter (fact 3), sitting inside a company that has a proven, quantified AI-personalisation mechanism (facts 2 and 4) and an explicit but unmeasured churn-defense objective (fact 1), where no version of that mechanism has yet been instrumented, measured, or reported for that specific market. The capstone's proposed opportunity is to fill that vacuum: an Egypt-specific AI-driven churn-prediction and early-tenure intervention capability, built on the same technical foundation talabat already operates elsewhere (the personalisation/ranking algorithm, the 6-order activation threshold, the ML-timed offer-surfacing logic used for talabat pro and Rewards), but pointed for the first time at the specific problem talabat's own 2026 language names — "churn to competition" — with Egypt's newly-visible, newly-profitable segment as the business case for making the investment now rather than later.

This is deliberately proposed as an *extension* of evidenced mechanisms, not an invented one: [[Advertising]]'s GEMs product already shows talabat operationalising AI-driven "win-back" logic for Partners with declining acquisition/retention; the recommendation here is the customer-side analogue talabat has not (per the corpus) built. Similarly, [[Customer Lifetime Value]]'s cohort-model inputs (retention/reorder rate, activity rate, average order size, commission rate) — disclosed as inputs to talabat's internal goodwill-impairment models but never as output values — give the capstone a structurally grounded, source-derived starting framework for what an Egypt churn-prediction model's feature set could look like, without needing to invent it from nothing.

Because no Egypt churn baseline, no Egypt personalisation-uplift figure, and no absolute Egypt LTV number exist in the corpus (per [[Current Pain Points]]), any quantified version of this proposal in the business plan — a projected churn-reduction percentage, a modelled retention-ROI figure, an illustrative Egypt LTV curve — must be constructed as synthetic/illustrative data, explicitly labeled as such per the project's standing instruction, and never presented as if it were a disclosed talabat figure. The proposal's strength is the evidenced logic connecting the four facts above; its numbers, where the plan needs numbers, are the capstone's own estimates.

## Evidence Basis
- [[Customer Churn]] — the near-total absence of any churn metric, and the one named-but-unmeasured "churn to competition" objective.
- [[Personalization]] and [[Recommendation Systems]] — the 6-order activation threshold and the inferred (not disclosed) elevated-churn-risk window it implies.
- [[Egypt]] — the segment's transition from bundled to standalone, its FY2025/Q1 2026 profitability swing, and talabat pro's February 2025 Egypt launch date.
- [[AI Opportunities]] — the Group-level, market-unattributed AI roadmap items this note proposes directing toward Egypt specifically.
- [[Advertising]] — the GEMs Partner-side win-back precedent used here as evidence of organisational familiarity with AI-driven win-back logic, not as evidence of an Egypt customer-side plan.
- [[Customer Lifetime Value]] — the cohort-model input structure (retention/reorder rate, activity rate, average order size, commission rate) used as a grounded starting framework, not a disclosed output.
- [[Current Pain Points]] — the consolidated gap list this note's opportunity is built directly on top of.

## Egypt-Specific Considerations
Every element of the proposal in this note is an extrapolation for Egypt, not a disclosed fact about Egypt. Egypt is chosen as the target of this recommendation precisely because it is the market with the least existing AI-retention disclosure and the most recent profitability inflection — the opposite of a market where this proposal could be validated against existing Egypt data. The business plan should present this note's argument as the rationale for *why Egypt is the right place to build and test this capability first*, not as evidence that it is already working or already planned there.

## Open Questions
- Whether talabat's internal cohort/goodwill-impairment models (which do reference retention/reorder-rate inputs) are computed for Egypt today in any form not disclosed to investors — genuinely unknown from this corpus.
- What data infrastructure would be required to build an Egypt-specific churn-prediction capability, given the corpus discloses no Egypt-level customer-record granularity at all.
- Whether talabat's existing personalisation/ranking algorithm (inherited substantially from Delivery Hero's shared stack, per [[AI]]) is technically extensible to a churn-prediction use case, or whether this would require new model development — not addressed anywhere in the corpus.
- Whether any pilot, test, or pre-existing internal initiative resembling this proposal already exists inside talabat Egypt — the corpus is silent, and this note does not assume either way.

## Strategic Implications
- This note is the direct evidentiary bridge into the business plan's core recommendation: propose an Egypt-specific AI-driven churn-prediction and early-tenure retention capability, explicitly framed as filling a gap talabat's own disclosures reveal rather than as a reported talabat initiative.
- The plan should use the GEMs precedent and the cohort-model input structure to argue technical and organisational feasibility (talabat has adjacent capabilities already), while using [[Current Pain Points]] to argue the case has not yet been made anywhere in the disclosed record.
- Any financial projection attached to this recommendation (churn-reduction percentage, retention ROI, Egypt LTV impact) must be built and labeled as the capstone's own synthetic estimate, consistent with the project's standing instruction on synthetic data.
- Because this is the vault's single most forward-looking note, it should be cited in the business plan's recommendation section as the capstone's synthesis — never footnoted as if it were a talabat-sourced claim.
