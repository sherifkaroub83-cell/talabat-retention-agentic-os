# RES-006 — GCC quick-commerce / online-grocery market outlook (top-down, two providers)

## Finding
Two named market-research providers independently size the **GCC quick-commerce market** (regional
GCC definition — see the geography caveat below) with strong but materially divergent growth
outlooks:

- **IMARC Group:** USD 2.7 billion (2025) → USD 26.5 billion (2034), CAGR 27.87% (2026–2034).
- **Mordor Intelligence:** USD 3.76 billion (2025) / USD 4.59 billion (2026) → USD 12.43 billion
  (2031), CAGR 22.05% (2026–2031). Segment detail (Mordor): grocery & staples led with **53.48% of
  the 2025 market**; **Saudi Arabia held 54.76% market share in 2025**; named major players include
  **Talabat Holding plc**, Noon AD Holdings, HungerStation, Careem Networks, and Jahez.
- Adjacent sizing (IMARC): **GCC online grocery** USD 9,156.8 million (2025) → USD 55,553.0 million
  (2034), CAGR 21.51% (2026–2034).

**What the two providers agree on:** a GCC quick-commerce market in the low-single-digit USD
billions today, compounding at >20% annually into the 2030s, with grocery as the dominant category —
directionally consistent with the corpus's own "low single-digit grocery penetration against a USD
150bn+ TAM" framing ([[Quick Commerce]], TLB-014/TLB-020). **What they disagree on:** the 2025 base
(USD 2.7bn vs USD 3.76bn, a ~28% gap) and the long-run trajectory (IMARC's path implies a far larger
market by the early 2030s than Mordor's).

## CRITICAL geography caveat (per Geographic_Evidence_Rules)
These reports use the **six-country regional GCC definition including Saudi Arabia**. talabat's
**GCC segment is five countries — UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman — and excludes Saudi Arabia**,
which per Mordor is ~54.76% of the regional market (served by sister DH brand HungerStation and by
Jahez, not talabat). **The headline figures above must never be quoted as talabat's addressable GCC
market.** A talabat-addressable derivation must strip the KSA share (leaving roughly 45% of the
regional headline as an upper bound) and be registered as an explicit assumption, not cited as a
provider figure.

## Source detail
IMARC Group "GCC Quick Commerce Market Size & Growth Forecast 2034" and "GCC Online Grocery Market"
report pages; Mordor Intelligence "GCC Quick Commerce Market Size, Share & 2031 Trends Report".
Both are modeled top-down estimates from commercial research firms; full methodology sections could
not be inspected this session (see Confidence rationale). Figures were captured 2026-07-24 from
WebSearch results directly attributed to the two providers' own report pages (and an IMARC press
release syndicated via OpenPR).

## Confidence rationale
**Medium.** Two acceptable, named providers independently corroborate the direction (>20% CAGR,
grocery-led) — but (1) they diverge materially on levels, so no single point estimate deserves
High; (2) direct retrieval of both report pages was blocked by this session's egress policy (HTTP
403 from the proxy for imarcgroup.com, mordorintelligence.com, and the OpenPR syndication), so the
figures rest on search-result content attributed to those pages, not full-page inspection; (3) both
are unaudited modeled estimates with uninspected methodologies. Any downstream use should quote the
two figures as a range, never average them into a false-precision midpoint.

## Limitations
- Regional GCC (incl. Saudi Arabia) ≠ talabat's GCC segment — see the geography caveat; no provider
  breakout for talabat's five GCC markets was retrievable.
- No Egypt, Jordan, or Iraq quick-commerce sizing was captured under this item (Egypt food-delivery
  sizing exists separately as RES-002/RES-003, pre-pivot, Egypt-only).
- Neither provider's figure is compatible-by-construction with talabat's own disclosed tMart GMV or
  the corpus's USD 150bn+ grocery TAM framing (different category boundaries: quick-commerce
  platforms vs total grocery retail) — do not mix them in one calculation without explicit bridging
  assumptions.
- Provider market definitions ("quick commerce" scope: delivery-speed threshold, verticals included)
  could not be verified against each other — a likely driver of the 2025 base-year divergence.
